TRICUSPID
Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Repair Reduces Death and Heart Failure Hospitalisation in Severe Tricuspid Regurgitation: The TRIC-I-HF Trial
ESC Congress 2026 / NEJM  ·  2026-08-31
Presented in a Hot Line session at ESC Congress 2026 on August 30, 2026 and published simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine, the investigator-initiated TRIC-I-HF-DZHK24 trial, conducted at 29 high-volume heart-valve centres in Germany, randomized 360 patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation and manifest right-sided heart failure (mean age 80.3, 56.4% women, 75% NYHA III/IV) 2:1 to transcatheter tricuspid valve intervention (TTVI, using CE-marked PASCAL/PASCAL ACE, TriClip, or Cardioband devices) plus optimal medical therapy (n=237) versus optimal medical therapy alone (n=123). Two hierarchically tested co-primary endpoints were used: a win-ratio composite of death, heart-failure hospitalisation, and quality of life at 1 year, and — tested only if the first reached significance — a composite of all-cause death or heart-failure hospitalisation at 3 years, for which event-free survival was significantly higher with TTVI (52.4% vs 21.0%; HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.29–0.55; P<0.001). This is the first randomized trial to show that transcatheter tricuspid repair reduces hard mortality/hospitalisation endpoints, filling a long-standing evidence gap in the field.

TAVI NEJM · 2026-09-03
Timing of PCI in Patients Undergoing Transcatheter Aortic-Valve Implantation: The TAVI PCI Trial
The investigator-initiated TAVI PCI trial, presented as an ESC Congress 2026 Hot Line and published simultaneously in NEJM (Stähli et al.), randomized 986 patients with severe aortic stenosis and concomitant coronary artery disease across 48 centers in Austria, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, and Switzerland to a TAVI-first or PCI-first strategy, with the second procedure performed 1-45 days later. At 1 year, the composite primary endpoint (death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, ischemia-driven revascularization, valve/procedure/heart-failure-related rehospitalization, and severe bleeding) occurred in 22.2% of the TAVI-first group versus 24.2% of the PCI-first group (difference -2.0 percentage points, 95% CI -7.4 to 3.4; P<0.001 for noninferiority), with severe bleeding lower in the TAVI-first arm (6.6% vs 9.7%). The results support a TAVI-first sequencing strategy as a safe default when both procedures are needed.
TRICUSPID NEJM · 2026-09-03
Tricuspid-Valve Intervention in Heart Failure: The TRIC-I-HF Trial
The German multicenter TRIC-I-HF (TRIC-I-HF-DZHK24) trial, led by Joerg Hausleiter and colleagues across 29 high-volume heart-valve centers, randomized 360 patients (mean age 80 years, 56% women) with symptomatic severe tricuspid regurgitation and heart failure to transcatheter tricuspid valve repair plus medical therapy versus medical therapy alone. Presented at ESC Congress 2026 and published simultaneously in NEJM, the trial met its first hierarchical primary endpoint (win-ratio composite of death, heart-failure hospitalization, and quality-of-life improvement at 1 year), with a win ratio of 2.42 (95% CI 1.76-3.33; P<0.001) favoring transcatheter repair; the co-primary endpoint of freedom from death or heart-failure hospitalization at 3 years was 52.4% with repair versus 21.0% with medical therapy alone.
TAVI ESC Congress 2026 / NEJM · 2026-08-31
TAVI-First Strategy Noninferior to PCI-First in Patients With Aortic Stenosis and Coronary Artery Disease: The TAVI-PCI Trial
This randomized controlled trial (TAVI-PCI), presented in a Hot Line session at ESC Congress 2026 on August 30, 2026 and published simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine, enrolled 986 patients with severe aortic stenosis and concomitant coronary artery disease across 48 centres in Austria, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, and Switzerland (mean age 82, 34% women). Patients were randomized 1:1 to TAVI followed by angiography-guided PCI, or PCI followed by TAVI, with both procedures completed within 1–45 days of each other. The primary endpoint — a composite of all-cause death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, ischaemia-driven revascularisation, valve-, procedure- or heart-failure-related rehospitalisation, or life-threatening, disabling or major bleeding at 1 year — occurred in 22.2% of the TAVI-first group versus 24.2% of the PCI-first group (risk difference −2.0 percentage points; 95% CI −7.4 to 3.4; P<0.001 for noninferiority), with no evidence of heterogeneity across prespecified subgroups including age, sex, and PCI complexity. The findings suggest PCI does not have to precede valve replacement in patients requiring both procedures, offering more flexibility in procedural scheduling.
TAVI JAMA · 2026-09-03
Anticoagulation Monotherapy vs Antiplatelet Monotherapy After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implant: The ACASA-TAVI Randomized Clinical Trial
The ACASA-TAVI randomized trial, presented at ESC Congress 2026 and published simultaneously in JAMA, enrolled 360 patients aged 65-80 undergoing TAVI for severe aortic stenosis with no other indication for anticoagulation, randomizing them to 12 months of direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) monotherapy versus aspirin 75 mg/day monotherapy. Hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT) on CT was less frequent with DOAC than with aspirin (16.2% vs 28.6%), while DOAC monotherapy was noninferior to aspirin for bleeding, thromboembolic events, and death, offering new randomized evidence on optimal antithrombotic strategy after TAVI.
TAVI JACC · 2026-09-02
Short-Term Anticoagulant Therapy and Subclinical Leaflet Thickening in Transcatheter Aortic Valves: The NOTION-4 Trial
The Danish NOTION-4 randomized trial, conducted at Rigshospitalet-Copenhagen University Hospital and Aarhus University Hospital and led by Troels Højsgaard Jørgensen and Ole De Backer, enrolled 324 TAVI patients with no standing indication for oral anticoagulation, randomizing them to a 3-month direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) course followed by lifelong single antiplatelet therapy versus lifelong single antiplatelet therapy alone. The primary endpoint was 1-year cardiac 4DCT-assessed hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT). DOAC therapy significantly reduced HALT incidence compared with antiplatelet therapy alone (RR 0.50, 95% CI 0.36-0.70, P=0.0001). Presented in an ESC Congress 2026 Hot Line session and published simultaneously in JACC, the trial adds new randomized evidence on periprocedural antithrombotic strategy for TAVI patients without a standing anticoagulation indication, complementing the concurrently reported ACASA-TAVI trial in patients already needing anticoagulation.
TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-09-04
Redo-Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement With Self-Expanding Valves: A U.S. Registry Analysis
Using the STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry, Rogers, Bapat and colleagues analyzed 439 patients undergoing redo-TAVR for a failed TAVR valve, all treated with supra-annular self-expanding Evolut R/PRO/PRO+ valves (mean age 78.9±9.8 years, 63.1% women), compared with a contemporary native-TAVR cohort on the same valve platform. After adjustment, redo-TAVR showed mortality and stroke rates comparable to native TAVR at both 30 days (mortality HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.39-1.13; stroke HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.54-1.68) and 1 year (mortality HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.72-1.15; stroke HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.63-1.34). Published August 24, 2026, in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, it is the largest real-world dataset to date on rescue strategies for TAVR valve failure.
OTHER Medscape · 2026-09-06
Cardioband Scrutiny Widens Across European Hospitals
The cardiac-surgery scandal at University Hospital Zurich (USZ) continues to widen: a 216-page independent commission report released in late April 2026 found that, based on statistical analysis of roughly 4,500 operations performed between 2016 and 2020 under then-department head Francesco Maisano, the unit's mortality was 68-74 cases higher than expected versus peer university hospitals -- attributing some deaths to use of the Cardioband transcatheter annuloplasty device (Edwards Lifesciences), which Maisano helped develop, in cases without clear clinical indication. Zurich prosecutors have opened a criminal investigation for suspected negligent homicide, negligent grievous bodily harm, and document forgery, and three hospital board members have resigned. In response, Bern's Inselspital has launched an independent review of its own 36 Cardioband cases (no deaths with a direct temporal link to the procedure found so far), while some German hospitals say they now use the device only selectively in complex tricuspid-regurgitation cases -- prompting broader reflection across structural heart interventions on indication selection, safety monitoring, and informed consent for novel devices.
TAVI ESC Congress 2026 · 2026-08-31
NOAC Monotherapy Nearly Halves Subclinical Leaflet Thrombosis Compared With Aspirin After TAVI: The ACASA-TAVI Trial
The ACASA-TAVI randomized trial, presented in a Hot Line session at ESC Congress 2026, randomized 360 patients undergoing TAVI with no indication for long-term anticoagulation 1:1 to 12 months of monotherapy with a factor Xa inhibitor NOAC (apixaban, edoxaban, or rivaroxaban) versus aspirin monotherapy. The primary efficacy endpoint, hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT) on cardiac CT at 12 months, occurred in 27 of 167 patients (16.2%) in the NOAC group versus 48 of 168 patients (28.6%) in the aspirin group (risk ratio 0.55; 95% CI 0.37–0.82; P=0.004); the NOAC arm was noninferior to aspirin for the composite safety endpoint of bleeding, thromboembolism, and death, with numerically more bleeding events — including all life-threatening or fatal bleeds — occurring in the aspirin group. The trial provides new randomized evidence for antithrombotic strategy after TAVI in patients without an anticoagulation indication, though whether reduced subclinical leaflet thrombosis translates into better long-term valve durability or fewer clinical thromboembolic events awaits confirmation in larger, longer-term studies.
LAA ESC Congress 2026 · 2026-09-01
Left Atrial Appendage Closure During Cardiac Surgery Benefits Only High Stroke-Risk Patients: The LAACS-2 Trial
Presented in a Hot Line session at ESC Congress 2026 on August 31, 2026, the LAACS-2 trial, led by Associate Professor Helena Dominguez of Bispebjerg University Hospital, Copenhagen, randomized 1,500 patients undergoing first-time planned open-heart surgery (with or without prior atrial fibrillation) 1:1 across four centres in Denmark, Spain, and Sweden to concomitant surgical left atrial appendage closure versus standard care (LAA left open). Over a median 4.0-year follow-up, the primary endpoint of stroke or transient ischaemic attack occurred at similar rates in both groups (4.28% vs 5.04%; HR 0.85; 95% CI 0.53-1.37; P=0.517), missing significance overall - but a prespecified subgroup analysis by CHA2DS2-VASc score found a 56% relative risk reduction with LAA closure among patients at high baseline stroke risk (P=0.018). The findings argue against routine LAA closure in all cardiac surgery patients, suggesting any benefit is confined to those at high stroke risk.
TAVI ESC Congress 2026 / Radcliffe Cardiology · 2026-09-06
POPular ACE TAVI: Routine vs Selective Protamine Administration to Reduce Bleeding After TAVI
Presented as a Hot Line trial at ESC Congress 2026, the investigator-initiated, double-blind, placebo-controlled POPular ACE TAVI trial (led by St Antonius Hospital, the Netherlands) randomized roughly 1,000 transfemoral TAVI patients (mean age 80) 1:1 to routine versus selective protamine administration before large-bore sheath removal. Routine protamine reduced the 30-day composite of all-cause death or clinically relevant bleeding by 10.9 percentage points (P<0.001), driven mainly by fewer minor bleeding events, but was associated with a higher rate of anaphylaxis (absolute risk difference 1.3 percentage points, P=0.01) -- a simple bleeding-reduction strategy that requires heart teams to prepare for occasional allergic reactions.
IMAGING DAIC (Diagnostic and Interventional Cardiology) · 2026-09-06
Heartvue.ai and Innolitics Achieve FDA 510(k) Clearance for Heartvue.Proton, an AI-Powered Cardiac MRI Analysis Platform
Brentwood, Tennessee-based Heartvue.ai, founded by Vanderbilt cardiologist Jeffrey Dendy, announced on September 2, 2026, that it and partner Innolitics received FDA 510(k) clearance (K260811) for Heartvue.Proton, an AI-powered platform for viewing, post-processing, and quantitative evaluation of cardiovascular MRI, achieved on the company's first FDA submission. The software automates 2D linear measurements, ventricular volume/ejection-fraction calculation, and blood-flow quantification, integrating into existing PACS/EHR workflows while keeping every result under physician review; the clearance includes an FDA-authorized Predetermined Change Control Plan allowing model updates within pre-specified bounds without new 510(k) filings, broadening AI-assisted quantitative imaging options relevant to structural heart evaluation.
