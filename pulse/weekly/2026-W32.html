Left Atrial Appendage Closure or Anticoagulation for Atrial Fibrillation
NEJM  ·  2026-08-08
The NEJM-published CHAMPION-AF randomized trial, presented at ACC 2026, reported 3-year outcomes in 3,000 atrial fibrillation patients eligible for oral anticoagulation (mean age 71.7, 31.9% women) randomized to Watchman FLX left atrial appendage closure (Boston Scientific) versus a NOAC. The composite of cardiovascular death, stroke, or systemic embolism occurred in 5.7% of the LAAC group versus 4.8% with NOACs, meeting the prespecified non-inferiority margin; annualized ischemic stroke/systemic embolism was 1.1%, and ISTH bleeding (including procedural) was reduced by 34% (12.8% vs 19.0% at 36 months). The trial is the first randomized evidence supporting LAAC as a potential first-line stroke-prevention option, though industry funding and lower-than-expected event rates have fueled debate over the strength of its non-inferiority claim.

TRICUSPID JAMA · 2026-08-07
Real-World Outcomes of Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Analysis From the STS/ACC TVT Registry
A JAMA-published analysis of the STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry evaluated 1,034 consecutive patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation who underwent transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR, all Evoque devices) at 82 US centers between February 2024 and March 2025 (mean age 77, 69% female, 11% Black). Valve implantation succeeded in over 98% of cases, TR was reduced to mild or less in nearly all patients by 30 days, and mortality, stroke, and bleeding outcomes were consistent with the TRISCEND II pivotal trial - with lower rates of new cardiac device implantation and bleeding than in the randomized trial - providing the first large-scale US real-world confirmation of safety and effectiveness since the device's 2024 FDA approval.
TAVI Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-09
Outcomes of Patients With New Left Bundle Branch Block After TAVR: TVT Registry Insights
An analysis of the STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry covering 202,533 TAVR recipients discharged alive between 2016 and 2022 found that 16.3% (32,933 patients) developed new left bundle branch block (LBBB), with incidence falling from 19.9% in Q1 2016 to 14.4% in Q3 2022. New LBBB was associated with a markedly higher rate of post-discharge permanent pacemaker implantation (7.1% vs 2.1%; adjusted HR 3.50; P<0.001), plus increased all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, rehospitalization, and worsening left ventricular systolic function, underscoring the need for closer rhythm monitoring in these patients.
TRICUSPID JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-05
The Less the Better: Mild vs Moderate Residual Tricuspid Regurgitation After T-TEER: Insights From EuroTR
The multicenter European EuroTR registry followed 2,545 patients (mean age 79, 54% women) who underwent tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) at 26 centers from 2016-2024 (baseline TR severe in 44%, massive in 33%, torrential in 20%). Even a single step-up in residual TR - from none/trace to mild, or mild to moderate - was linked to significantly lower 2-year survival and survival free from heart failure hospitalization, arguing that procedural success should target near-none residual TR rather than the conventional "mild or less" threshold.
TAVI JACC · 2026-08-08
Six-Year Outcomes After Transcatheter vs Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement in Low-Risk Patients With Aortic Stenosis
Six-year outcomes from the randomized Evolut Low Risk trial, published in JACC, followed 1,414 low-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis enrolled in 2016-2019 comparing self-expanding TAVR (Medtronic Evolut) with surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR). The primary endpoint of all-cause mortality or disabling stroke showed no significant difference through 6 years, though the survival curves crossed at 5 years to numerically favor surgery. Reintervention was 5.5% with TAVR versus 3.3% with SAVR at 6 years (p=0.07), widening to a statistically significant 9.8% versus 6.0% when follow-up extended to 7 years - driven mainly by bioprosthetic aortic regurgitation rather than valve stenosis (5.6% vs 1.6%), a signal that longer-term reintervention risk should factor into TAVR decisions for younger, low-risk patients.
IMAGING NewYork-Presbyterian / Columbia · 2026-08-03
AI Tool Developed at NewYork-Presbyterian and Columbia to Detect Hidden Heart Disease Receives FDA Approval
EchoNext, an AI tool developed at NewYork-Presbyterian and Columbia University (led by Dr. Pierre Elias) and commercialized by Pathway Labs, received FDA clearance as the world's first AI tool to screen for hidden structural heart disease from a standard 12-lead ECG, flagging valve disease, right/left heart failure, severe hypertrophy suggestive of infiltrative cardiomyopathy, and pulmonary hypertension. Trained on 1.2 million paired ECG-echocardiogram studies from 230,000 patients and validated across more than 20 hospitals and 500,000 patients, EchoNext outperformed cardiologists (with or without AI assistance) at detecting structural heart disease; the underlying Nature study reported 77% accuracy for EchoNext versus 64% for cardiologists reading ECGs alone.
LAA EuroIntervention · 2026-08-07
Left Atrial Appendage Closure With the AnchorMan or Watchman Devices: 12-Month Results of the SAFE-PROTECT Randomised Trial
The multicenter, open-label, non-inferiority SAFE-PROTECT randomized trial, published in EuroIntervention, enrolled 216 patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation at high stroke risk (mean CHA2DS2-VASc ~4.0), randomized 1:1 to left atrial appendage closure with MicroPort's next-generation AnchorMan device or the Watchman 2.5. At 12 months, clinical success (freedom from ischemic/hemorrhagic stroke, systemic embolism, and cardiovascular or unexplained death) was 98.1% in both arms, meeting the prespecified non-inferiority margin; both devices achieved 100% procedural occlusion success (peri-device leak <5 mm), while AnchorMan showed a significantly lower rate of peri-device leak >3 mm than Watchman - positioning this Chinese-developed device as a competitive alternative on both efficacy and leak control.
MITRAL EHJ-VSHD · 2026-08-08
Prognostic impact of tricuspid regurgitation improvement after transcatheter mitral valve replacement: results from an international multicentre registry
The CHOICE-MI international multicenter registry, published in European Heart Journal - Valvular and Structural Heart Disease, analyzed 309 patients (median age 77) with moderate-to-severe mitral regurgitation treated with dedicated transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR) devices across 31 centers. Tricuspid regurgitation (TR) improved by at least one grade in 33.0% of patients after TMVR, and this improvement independently predicted lower 1-year all-cause mortality or heart failure hospitalization and greater symptomatic benefit - underscoring the prognostic relevance of concomitant TR when planning and following TMVR.
TAVI Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-09
Impact of TAVR Failure Mechanism on Outcomes After Reintervention: From the EXPLANTORREDO-TAVR Registry
The EXPLANTORREDO-TAVR international multicenter registry analyzed 553 patients across 29 centers (mean age 75.6±9.3, 42% women) who underwent surgical explant or redo-TAVR for transcatheter valve failure between 2009 and 2022. Structural valve deterioration (SVD) accounted for 64.9% of failures and nonstructural valve dysfunction (NSVD, mainly paravalvular leak and prosthesis-patient mismatch) for 35.1%. Time from index TAVR to reintervention was far longer with SVD than NSVD (50.7 vs 5.5 months; P<0.001), and SVD was more common with balloon-expandable valves (50.7% vs 24.8%; P<0.001), while overall choice of reintervention strategy (explant vs redo-TAVR) did not differ significantly between groups — suggesting failure mechanism should help guide the reintervention approach.
TRICUSPID Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-09
Invasive Hemodynamics and Risk Stratification in T-TEER: Moving Beyond ESC Thresholds — EuroTR Registry Insights
An analysis of 711 patients from the European Registry of Transcatheter Repair for Tricuspid Regurgitation (EuroTR) undergoing T-TEER found 2-year survival free of death or heart failure hospitalization (HFH) of 63%. The study identified optimal prognostic thresholds — mean pulmonary artery pressure ≥32 mmHg and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) ≥20 mmHg — that exceeded standard ESC pulmonary hypertension diagnostic cutoffs. PCWP ≥20 mmHg independently predicted early clinical deterioration (HR 2.77) and 2-year death/HFH (HR 1.75), suggesting risk stratification in advanced TR should use population-specific hemodynamic thresholds rather than general pulmonary hypertension guideline criteria.
MITRAL JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-05
Balloon-Assisted Translocation of the Mitral Anterior Leaflet to Prevent Outflow Tract Obstruction During TMVR: BATMAN-Registry
The BATMAN-Registry, simultaneously published in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions at EuroPCR 2026, enrolled 83 patients at high risk of left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction who underwent transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR) using the balloon-assisted translocation of the anterior mitral leaflet (BATMAN) technique between 2023 and 2026. Technical success was 98.8%, and 95.1% of patients were free from LVOT obstruction or procedural death, adding evidence for a bailout strategy that could broaden TMVR eligibility in anatomically high-risk patients.
LAA JACC: Advances · 2026-08-05
MAUDE Registry Analysis of Pericardial Effusions Following Watchman and Amulet Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion
A retrospective analysis of the FDA MAUDE adverse-event database identified 290 pericardial effusion reports associated with the Amulet occluder (66% acute, 34% late) versus 33 with Watchman (94% acute, 6% late) through December 2024. Among Amulet cases, 65% of acute and 55% of late effusions required intervention (pericardiocentesis, pericardial window, or sternotomy), with mortality of 8.4% and 8.0%, respectively - a reminder that late pericardial effusion, though less common, carries meaningful risk and merits longer-term post-LAAO surveillance.
