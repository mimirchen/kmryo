Left Atrial Appendage Closure or Anticoagulation for Atrial Fibrillation
NEJM  ·  2026-08-24
CHAMPION-AF, the largest randomized trial testing left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) as a first-line stroke-prevention strategy, enrolled 3,000 patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation at 141 sites across the US, Canada, Europe, Japan, and Australia, randomizing them to the Watchman FLX device or a NOAC. At 3 years, LAAC was noninferior to NOACs for the primary efficacy endpoint of cardiovascular death, stroke, or systemic embolism (5.7% vs 4.8%) and superior for non-procedure-related major/clinically relevant bleeding (10.9% vs 19.0%, a 45% relative reduction, P<0.001), with a favorable net clinical benefit (15.1% vs 21.8%, P<0.001). Published simultaneously in NEJM, the results support LAAC as a reasonable first-line alternative to anticoagulation even in patients who can tolerate NOACs, though some experts caution about weighing device-related complications against the long-term benefit.

TAVI Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-25
Balloon- Versus Self-Expanding Transcatheter Valves for Failed Small Surgical Aortic Bioprostheses: 3-Year Results of the LYTEN Trial
The randomized LYTEN trial compared balloon-expandable (SAPIEN 3/ULTRA, n=46) and self-expanding (Evolut R/PRO/PRO+, n=52) transcatheter valves for valve-in-valve TAVR in 98 patients with failed small (≤23mm) surgical aortic bioprostheses. At 3 years, a mean gradient ≥20 mmHg occurred in only 17.6% of self-expanding-valve patients versus 62.1% of balloon-expandable-valve patients (P<0.001), and intended valve performance was met in 82.4% versus 27.6% (P<0.001), with similar clinical outcomes between groups. Published in Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions, these are the longest randomized follow-up data yet on device selection for small failed surgical valves, showing the self-expanding valve's hemodynamic advantage persists through 3 years.
TRICUSPID JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-24
Incidence, Clinical Implications, and Predictors of Paravalvular Leak Following Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: The TRIPLACE Registry
The multicenter TRIPLACE registry stratified 394 patients undergoing transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) by predischarge paravalvular leak (PVL) severity: 71.6% had none-or-trace, 22.3% mild, and 6.1% (n=24) moderate-or-severe PVL. Moderate-or-severe PVL was associated with significantly higher 1-year all-cause mortality and worse functional status; larger right atrial volume, device malposition, and type IV annular morphology were independent predictors. Published in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions (2026;19:680-693), the findings suggest PVL may attenuate the clinical benefit of TTVR and highlight a target for future device and technique refinement.
TAVI JACC · 2026-08-26
Six-Year Outcomes After Transcatheter vs Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement in Low-Risk Patients With Aortic Stenosis
The randomized Evolut Low Risk trial compared self-expanding transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) with surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) in low-surgical-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis. At 6 years, the primary composite endpoint of all-cause mortality or disabling stroke was similar between arms (TAVR 23.3% vs SAVR 20.4%, P=0.43), but reintervention trended higher with TAVR (5.5% vs 3.3%, P=0.07) and became significantly higher in the subset with 7-year data (9.8% vs 6.0%). The excess reintervention was driven largely by bioprosthetic aortic regurgitation (TAVR 5.6% vs SAVR 1.6%; subdistribution HR 3.39, 95% CI 1.62-7.07, P<0.001) rather than stenosis (3.6% vs 3.5%, P=0.70). Published online in JACC in February 2026, these are the longest randomized follow-up data yet for low-risk TAVR, flagging regurgitation-related valve failure as a signal to watch long term.
TAVI JAMA Cardiology · 2026-08-30
Seven-Year Valve Durability With Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement: An Ad Hoc Analysis of the PARTNER 3 Randomized Clinical Trial
This ad hoc analysis of the PARTNER 3 randomized trial, published online in JAMA Cardiology on June 24, 2026 (DOI 10.1001/jamacardio.2026.2299), followed 1,000 low-surgical-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis (495 TAVR, 453 SAVR) for 7-year valve durability. Structural valve deterioration occurred at similar rates between groups (TAVR 7.3% vs SAVR 7.6%), bioprosthetic valve failure was 6.9% vs 7.5%, and valve-related reintervention was needed in 6% vs 5.5%; about 75% of patients in both arms were alive with a fully functioning valve at 7 years. Thrombosis-related bioprosthetic valve dysfunction was more common after TAVR, but the authors note these events tended to occur early, were often reversible, and rarely progressed to failure or reintervention. The findings provide the longest-term durability evidence yet supporting TAVR in younger, lower-risk patients.
TAVI Circulation · 2026-08-26
Three-Year Follow-Up of the NOTION-2 Trial: TAVR Versus SAVR to Treat Younger Low-Risk Patients With Tricuspid or Bicuspid Aortic Stenosis
NOTION-2 is the first randomized trial dedicated to TAVR versus SAVR in patients aged 60-75 years at low surgical risk with severe aortic stenosis, including both bicuspid and tricuspid valve anatomy — a population excluded from earlier low-risk trials. Among 370 randomized patients (mean age 71.1 years, mean STS-PROM 1.2%), the 3-year primary composite endpoint (death, stroke, or procedure-, valve-, or heart failure-related hospitalization) occurred in 16.1% of TAVR patients versus 12.6% of SAVR patients (HR 1.3, 95% CI 0.8-2.2), a nonsignificant difference; outcomes were nearly identical in the tricuspid-valve subgroup (14.5% vs 14.4%). Rates of structural valve deterioration and reintervention were low in both arms. Published in Circulation, the trial gives the first randomized evidence on device choice for younger, bicuspid-inclusive low-risk patients, though longer follow-up is still needed to settle durability questions.
IMAGING Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-29
Pre-TAVI CT Angiography for Coronary Artery Disease Assessment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Clinical Outcomes
This systematic review and meta-analysis, published in Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions (January 2026), evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of pre-TAVI coronary CT angiography (CCTA) for obstructive coronary artery disease. In an intention-to-diagnose analysis, CCTA sensitivity was 97% (95% CI 94-98%) and specificity 68% (95% CI 56-68%), with a positive likelihood ratio of 3.0, negative likelihood ratio of 0.05, and an area under the hierarchical summary ROC curve of 0.96 (95% CI 0.94-0.98). Single-heartbeat CT scanners showed similar sensitivity to other scanner types (96% vs 97%) but markedly higher specificity (82% vs 60%). Assuming a 40% disease prevalence, routine pre-TAVI CCTA could avoid 41% (95% CI 34-47%) of invasive coronary angiograms, supporting a CT-first, selectively-confirmed pathway for pre-TAVI coronary assessment.
TRICUSPID JACC · 2026-08-25
Operator and Institutional Recommendations and Requirements for Tricuspid Interventions: 2026 ACC/AHA/ASE/HRS/STS Expert Consensus Systems of Care Document
The American College of Cardiology, American Heart Association, American Society of Echocardiography, Heart Rhythm Society, and Society of Thoracic Surgeons jointly published a 2026 expert consensus systems-of-care document on tricuspid interventions in JACC. It requires institutions launching transcatheter tricuspid valve intervention (TTVI) programs to have comprehensive imaging and therapeutic facilities plus an established cardiac surgery program with prior experience in PCI, TAVI, mitral TEER, balloon valvuloplasty, and closure devices; transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) should initially involve a three-operator team (interventional echocardiographer, interventional cardiologist, cardiac surgeon), which may be streamlined only after 25 cases or 3 years of experience with a demonstrated 30-day major complication rate below 20%; and centers must participate in the STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy Registry for postmarket surveillance. It is the first multisociety systems-of-care standard specifically for tricuspid intervention program credentialing and team composition.
MITRAL JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-27
Clinical Outcomes of Transfemoral Transcatheter Mitral Valve Replacement: Results From the CHOICE-MI Registry
The multicenter CHOICE-MI registry analyzed 124 patients (mean age 79, 50% female, mean EuroSCORE II 5.0%) undergoing transfemoral transseptal transcatheter mitral valve replacement across 9 different devices, in a population with 39.5% primary mitral regurgitation and 12.9% mitral annular calcification. Technical success was achieved in 91.1% (113/124), with durable, predictable resolution of mitral regurgitation and functional improvement in most patients, tracked against a 2-year composite of all-cause mortality or heart failure hospitalization. Published in the August 2026 issue of JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, this is the largest real-world dataset to date on the transfemoral approach to TMVR, for which evidence has previously been scarce.
IMAGING JACC · 2026-08-28
AI-ECG Detection of Structural Heart Disease in the Community Setting: Transportability and Spectrum Effects in the PREVUE-VALVE Study
This research letter published in JACC (Vol. 88, Issue 7, Aug 18 2026, pp. 765-767) externally validated EchoNext, an AI-ECG model originally trained on a multicenter hospital-based cohort to detect structural heart disease (SHD), in the community-based PREVUE-VALVE cohort (2,402 community-dwelling adults aged 65-85 undergoing in-home ECG and echocardiography). SHD prevalence was far lower in the community setting (8% vs 43% in hospital cohorts), with a milder disease spectrum. AI-ECG performance dropped accordingly — AUC fell from 83% in the hospital derivation cohort to 71% in PREVUE-VALVE, with a positive predictive value of just 14%, meaning most positive results did not correspond to moderate-or-greater SHD. The findings underscore the need to validate AI screening tools within their intended-use population before community deployment.
TRICUSPID European Heart Journal · 2026-08-29
Combined Transcatheter Mitral and Tricuspid Edge-to-Edge Repair or Tricuspid Edge-to-Edge Repair Alone in Moderate Mitral Regurgitation: A Propensity-Matched Analysis
This propensity-matched analysis from the EuroTR registry (2016-2025) examined 2,762 patients undergoing transcatheter tricuspid edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) for severe tricuspid regurgitation. Multivariable analysis showed concomitant moderate mitral regurgitation (MR) predicted higher mortality (HR 1.81, P=0.005), while concomitant mitral TEER (M-TEER) was associated with a survival benefit (HR 0.46, P<0.0001). After matching (217 pairs), patients receiving concomitant M-TEER had greater TR reduction (-1.9 vs -1.6 grades), better NYHA class and 6-minute walk distance improvement, and higher survival at 1 year (87% vs 76%) and 2 years (81% vs 70%). Published in the European Heart Journal (Aug 7, 2026; Vol. 47, Issue 30, pp. 4113-4128), the findings support treating concomitant moderate MR at the time of T-TEER in patients with multivalve disease.
LAA MedTech Dive · 2026-08-30
Abbott's Next-Generation Amulet 360 Left Atrial Appendage Occluder Receives CE Mark for Stroke Prevention in Atrial Fibrillation
On August 27, 2026, Abbott announced that its next-generation dual-seal Amulet 360 left atrial appendage occluder received CE Mark approval to reduce stroke risk in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation. The device's lobe was redesigned to be softer and more conformable to treat a wider range of LAA anatomies, while its outer disk provides a second sealing layer to further reduce residual leak. The clearance builds on previously reported multicentre VERITAS study data: among 400 patients, implant success was 99.8%, and 93.9% achieved complete LAA closure without a leak greater than 3 mm at 45 days, with no reported additional surgery, stroke, or thrombus within 7 days. The first European cases were completed in Frankfurt, Lübeck, and Aarhus, with Abbott planning further rollout across Europe in the coming weeks. Amulet 360 will now compete directly with Boston Scientific's market-leading Watchman occluders.
