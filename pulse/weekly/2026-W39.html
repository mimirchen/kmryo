Real-World Outcomes of Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Analysis From the STS/ACC TVT Registry
JAMA  ·  2026-09-24
Drawing on the US STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry, this study analyzed 1,034 consecutive patients (mean age 77, 69% female, 75% NYHA III/IV at baseline) who underwent transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR, EVOQUE system) at 82 US centers between February 2024 and March 2025. Tricuspid regurgitation was reduced to mild or less in 98.4% of patients post-procedure and 97.7% at 30 days; 30-day all-cause mortality was 3.1%, stroke 0.2%, major bleeding 7.9%, and heart failure hospitalization 3.1%, while 82.7% reached NYHA class I/II and mean KCCQ overall summary score improved by 22.4 points (all P<.001). The real-world registry results closely mirror the pivotal TRISCEND II trial, providing strong evidence supporting broader adoption of TTVR.

TRICUSPID JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-09-21
Frequency and Outcomes of Acute Hemodynamic Instability Following Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Insights From the CESAR-TR-Registry
The international multicenter CESAR-TR registry retrospectively analyzed 200 high-surgical-risk patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation who underwent transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) across 5 centers (mean age 78±11 years, 64% women), examining the frequency, outcomes, and risk factors of acute hemodynamic instability (AHI) after the procedure. AHI occurred in 8.5% of patients, always within 24 hours post-procedure, and was associated with markedly higher in-hospital mortality (35.2% vs 0.1% without AHI; P<0.001); among AHI patients, 65% presented with SCAI stage C cardiogenic shock, 6% stage D, and 29% stage E. Elevated left-sided filling pressures, pulmonary hypertension, and impaired renal function independently predicted AHI risk, underscoring the importance of periprocedural hemodynamic monitoring and careful patient selection for TTVR.
TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-09-22
Surgical Bailout During TAVR: A Contemporary Analysis of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Database
Using the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) database, this contemporary US analysis examined 400,862 TAVR procedures performed between 2016 and 2024, of which 2,240 (0.56%) required emergent surgical bailout for procedural complications; the incidence fell significantly from 0.92% in 2016 to 0.40% in 2024. Despite this decline, outcomes after bailout remained poor: 30-day mortality was 26.3% versus 1.8% in patients who did not require surgery, with elevated mortality persisting at 1 year (35% vs 10%) and 5 years (48% vs 26%). The most common bailout interventions were mediastinal hematoma evacuation (38%), ventricular repair (20%), surgical aortic valve replacement (16%), and coronary artery bypass grafting (13%), underscoring the continued importance of heart-team readiness and on-site surgical backup even as TAVR technology matures.
LAA JACC: Asia · 2026-09-21
Effect of Device Size on Outcomes Following Left Atrial Appendage Closure in Borderline-Sized Anatomy
Using data from Japan's multicenter TERMINATOR registry (1,190 patients who underwent second-generation WATCHMAN FLX left atrial appendage closure between May 2021 and September 2023), this substudy focused on 757 patients with borderline-sized LAA ostial diameters (16.8-18, 18.9-21.6, 21.7-24.3, or 24.5-27.9 mm) for whom two device sizes were anatomically viable, comparing outcomes of larger versus smaller device selection. Choosing the larger device in borderline anatomy significantly reduced ischemic stroke or systemic embolism and was associated with fewer peri-device leaks, without increasing procedural risk — offering practical guidance for device sizing in borderline LAA cases.
TAVI JACC: Asia · 2026-09-22
New-Generation Transcatheter Valves for Extremely Small Aortic Annuli
Drawing on Japan's multicenter OCEAN-TAVI registry, this study compared early clinical and hemodynamic outcomes among next-generation balloon-expandable valves (BEV), supra-annular self-expanding valves (SA-SEV), and intra-annular self-expanding valves (IA-SEV) in patients with severe aortic stenosis and extremely small aortic annuli (annular area <314 mm²). Adjusted rates of severe prosthesis-patient mismatch were low and similar across the three valve types (1.6%, 2.0%, and 2.7% respectively), though ≥mild paravalvular leak was numerically more frequent with self-expanding valves. The findings suggest broadly comparable early performance among contemporary transcatheter valve platforms in this technically challenging small-annulus subgroup, offering real-world guidance for device selection.
MITRAL JACC: Asia · 2026-09-22
Novel Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair System for Degenerative Mitral Regurgitation: 1-Year Outcomes
The GeminiOne-DMR pivotal trial evaluated Peijia Medical's domestically developed GeminiOne transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) system in 120 surgically prohibitive-risk patients with degenerative mitral regurgitation (DMR) grade ≥3+, assessed by an independent echocardiography core lab and clinical events committee. At 1 year, clinical success reached 89.84%±2.78%, meeting the prespecified primary efficacy endpoint; freedom from all-cause mortality, major adverse events, mitral valve reintervention, and heart-failure hospitalization was 97.48%, 91.61%, 98.31%, and 94.00% respectively, with 92.24% of patients achieving MR ≤2+ and NYHA class I/II rising from 23.33% at baseline to 94.74% at 1 year. The results provide positive 1-year efficacy and safety evidence for this Chinese-designed TEER device, which features a distinctive sliding-groove grasping mechanism.
MITRAL JACC: Asia · 2026-09-23
Novel Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair System for High-Risk Primary Mitral Regurgitation: 2-Year Pivotal Trial Outcomes
This prospective, multicenter, single-arm, performance-goal pivotal trial enrolled 123 surgically high-risk patients with moderate-to-severe or severe primary mitral regurgitation across 23 Chinese centers (April 2022–March 2024) to evaluate the novel Neonova transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) system. The primary efficacy endpoint — 1-year freedom from death, mitral valve surgery, and MR >2+ — was met in 92.68% of patients, exceeding the prespecified 60% performance goal; the composite endpoint was sustained at 84.55% at 2 years, with 95.41% of patients achieving MR ≤2+. The results provide positive 2-year evidence for this Chinese-developed TEER system.
MITRAL JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-09-24
Clinical Outcomes of Transfemoral Transcatheter Mitral Valve Replacement: Results From the CHOICE-MI Registry
This international multicenter CHOICE-MI registry study enrolled 124 high-risk patients with mitral regurgitation (median age 79, 50% female, EuroSCORE II 5.0%, 39.5% primary MR) undergoing transfemoral transseptal transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TF TMVR) with 9 different dedicated devices, evaluating clinical, echocardiographic, and functional outcomes. Technical success was achieved in 91.1% (113/124); residual MR ≤1+ was present in 94.9% at discharge and remained stable at follow-up; cardiovascular mortality was 14.7% at 1 year and 20.3% at 2 years, while the primary composite endpoint of all-cause mortality or heart failure hospitalization occurred in 44.1% at 1 year and 52.4% at 2 years. Atrial fibrillation independently predicted the primary endpoint, while technical success was associated with survival — providing one of the largest international registry datasets to date on dedicated TF TMVR devices in real-world high-risk patients.
TAVI JACC: Advances · 2026-09-27
Long-Term Survival After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement Relative to an Age- and Comorbidity-Matched Population
In this study by Akiva Rosenzveig, Grant W. Reed and Cleveland Clinic colleagues, published in JACC: Advances, the authors used the TriNetX federated EHR network (roughly 113 million patients across 64 U.S. health systems) to build age-, sex- and comorbidity-matched cohorts comparing long-term survival after TAVR with matched populations without aortic stenosis. Among adults ≥65 years, 5-year all-cause mortality after TAVR was 14.9%, actually lower than 18.0% in matched controls (P<.001), suggesting TAVR has closed — and in this cohort reversed — the survival gap for older patients. However, among younger patients aged 50-64, 5-year mortality after TAVR was 16.2% versus 10.6% in the matched population (P<.001), indicating a persistent excess-mortality gap in younger, likely higher-comorbidity-burden TAVR recipients.
OTHER CJC Open · 2026-09-23
Patent Foramen Ovale Closure for Primary Prevention of Stroke
Using the US TriNetX real-world database, this retrospective cohort study identified adults diagnosed with patent foramen ovale (PFO) between 2014 and 2022 without prior ischemic stroke or atrial fibrillation, comparing those who underwent transcatheter PFO closure with those who did not (3,398 patients per propensity-matched cohort). Closure was associated with significantly lower ischemic stroke incidence (3.5% vs 6.4%) and lower all-cause hospitalization (15.5% vs 28.6%), though new-onset atrial fibrillation was slightly more common after closure (1.1% vs 0.4%); all-cause mortality did not differ significantly. Current guidelines do not recommend routine primary-prevention closure in PFO patients without a prior stroke, and this study offers new real-world evidence for that unaddressed scenario, though the absence of randomized data warrants cautious interpretation pending prospective trials.
TAVI Structural Heart · 2026-09-26
Sex-Specific Differences in Clinical Outcomes After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement: A Meta-Analysis of Reconstructed Individual Patient Survival Data
Published in Structural Heart by Ali M. Abdelaziz, Mamas A. Mamas and colleagues, this meta-analysis of reconstructed individual patient survival data pooled 71 studies comprising 481,353 TAVR patients to compare outcomes by sex. Women had significantly higher 30-day all-cause mortality (HR 1.064, 95% CI 1.005-1.127, P=.033) and stroke/TIA risk (HR 1.511, 95% CI 1.317-1.734, P<.001), plus higher rates of life-threatening bleeding (RR 1.29), major bleeding (RR 1.26), and major vascular complications (RR 1.66) than men. Paradoxically, women showed lower long-term all-cause mortality on extended follow-up — the so-called "female paradox" in TAVR. The findings argue for sex-tailored periprocedural risk mitigation, while the mechanism behind women's long-term survival edge remains unresolved.
OTHER JACC: Case Reports · 2026-09-24
Percutaneous Closure of a Giant Secundum Atrial Septal Defect Using 2 Sutured Occluders
Published in JACC: Case Reports, this case describes a 75-year-old man with a giant secundum atrial septal defect (64.5 × 38.8 mm) plus severe mitral and tricuspid regurgitation, pulmonary hypertension, atrial fibrillation, cirrhosis, and chronic kidney disease, rendering him prohibitive surgical risk. After staged transcatheter edge-to-edge repair of the mitral and tricuspid valves, the team sutured two 38-mm septal occluder devices together ex vivo and deployed them simultaneously, successfully closing a defect exceeding the size limits of any single available occluder, with a stable device position and no residual shunt. The case demonstrates a feasible percutaneous alternative for exceptionally large ASDs that traditionally required surgical repair.
