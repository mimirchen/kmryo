Left Atrial Appendage Closure or Medical Therapy in Atrial Fibrillation
NEJM  ·  2026-08-10
Published in NEJM, the CLOSURE-AF trial (Landmesser et al) randomized 912 atrial fibrillation patients at high risk of both stroke (mean CHA2DS2-VASc 5.2) and bleeding (mean HAS-BLED 3.0) across 42 German centers to left atrial appendage closure (n=446) or physician-directed intensified medical therapy including NOACs (n=442). Over a median 3-year follow-up, the intention-to-treat annualized rate of the primary composite endpoint (stroke, systemic embolism, cardiovascular/unexplained death, or major bleeding) was 16.83 per 100 patient-years with LAAC versus 13.27 with medical therapy (adjusted HR 1.28, 95% CI 1.01-1.62), missing the prespecified noninferiority margin of 1.3. The negative result stands in sharp contrast to the nearly concurrent CHAMPION-AF trial, underscoring how patient selection — especially in older, multimorbid populations — may determine whether LAAC outperforms medical therapy, and has already prompted calls for cautious interpretation in the European Heart Journal.

TAVI JACC · 2026-08-11
10-Year Outcomes of SAPIEN 3 Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement or Surgery in Intermediate-Risk Patients
Published in JACC (2026;87:3296-3308), this propensity-score-matched analysis compared 783 intermediate-surgical-risk patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis who received SAPIEN 3 TAVR in the PARTNER 2 SAPIEN 3 Intermediate-Risk Registry (P2S3i) against 783 matched patients who underwent SAVR in the PARTNER 2A randomized trial (mean age 82, 43% women, mean STS score 5.5%). At 10 years, TAVR and surgery showed similar rates of mortality and aortic valve reintervention, with comparable hemodynamic stability — among the longest follow-up data yet for SAPIEN 3 durability — though the authors note differential loss to follow-up and competing mortality risk in this elderly population complicate interpretation of such extended-term data.
TAVI JACC · 2026-08-12
Health Status Outcomes 7 Years After Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement in Low-Surgical Risk Patients With Aortic Stenosis
Published in JACC, this 7-year health-status analysis of the PARTNER 3 randomized trial (943 low-surgical-risk patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis; 494 TAVR, 449 SAVR) using the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ-OS) found TAVR conferred substantially higher scores at 1 month (mean difference +16.2 points) and modestly higher scores through 2 years (+1.9 points), with no significant between-group difference from years 3 through 7. At 7 years, roughly 60% of patients in both arms achieved an "excellent outcome" (alive, KCCQ-OS ≥75, no ≥10-point decline from baseline) -- confirming faster early recovery with TAVR but comparable long-term patient-reported health status versus surgery.
OTHER JACC · 2026-08-14
Population Prevalence of Valvular Heart Disease in the United States: The PREVUE-VALVE Study
The PREVUE-VALVE study used a decentralized, representative sampling approach -- randomly recruiting 3,000 US adults aged 65-85 via electronic invitation and mail for standardized echocardiographic screening -- to determine the true population prevalence of valvular heart disease (VHD). Moderate-or-greater VHD was found in 8.2% of participants, rising to 18.4% when mild-moderate disease was included; tricuspid regurgitation was most common (3.7%), followed by aortic stenosis (3.1%), mitral regurgitation (2.0%), and aortic regurgitation (0.7%). Extrapolated to current US demographics, this implies at least 4.7 million people living with moderate VHD and 10.6 million with clinically significant VHD -- a substantially higher burden than prior clinic-based estimates, offering key epidemiological grounding for expanding screening programs.
TAVI Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-12
Percutaneous Aorto-Coronary Bypass Graft to Prevent Coronary Obstruction Following TAVR: First Human VECTOR Procedure
In a case report published in Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions, a team from the US National Institutes of Health and Emory University performed the world's first human VECTOR (ventriculo-coronary transcatheter outward navigation and re-entry) procedure: using a covered stent punched through to connect the left main coronary artery and the aorta, they created a percutaneous aorto-coronary bypass that successfully prevented coronary obstruction in a TAVR-candidate patient ineligible for existing leaflet-modification techniques (BASILICA, UNICORN, CATHEDRAL, or snorkel stenting). The patient, who had previously undergone transcatheter mitral valve replacement, remained well 6 months later on lifelong oral anticoagulation plus single antiplatelet therapy -- an "outside-the-box" option for the rare high-risk patients left without a percutaneous protection strategy.
MITRAL JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-10
Durability of Mitral Valve Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair: An Expert Overview
This expert overview in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, authored by Mariani, Stolz, von Bardeleben and colleagues, synthesizes long-term durability data on mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER): pooled weighted rates of MR recurrence and reintervention were 5%-10% in the first months post-procedure, stayed roughly stable through 3 years, then rose to 17% and 9% respectively by 5 years. Recurrence and reintervention were numerically higher with primary than secondary MR, with 41% of primary-MR patients experiencing recurrence or needing reintervention by 5 years. For failed M-TEER, redo TEER appeared safer than surgery but still carried a 20%-40% 1-year MR recurrence rate, while mitral valve replacement was the most common surgical fallback — highlighting mid-to-long-term durability, particularly in primary MR, as an unresolved challenge for the field.
TAVI JACC: Asia · 2026-08-10
Early Safety and Feasibility Study of a Novel Transcatheter Aortic Valve With Polymeric Leaflets
Published in JACC: Asia, this first-in-human, prospective, single-arm, multicenter feasibility study evaluated the Sikelia transcatheter aortic valve — the world's first self-expanding TAVR device with dip-coated polyurethane composite leaflets instead of conventional bovine/porcine pericardial tissue. Between 2022 and 2024, four high-volume centers in China implanted the valve in 12 patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis (mean age ~74, 75% men, mean STS score 4.51%), with encouraging 1-year outcomes and no valve-related safety signals. Investigators say the polymeric leaflets' greater flexibility could improve paravalvular sealing while reducing thrombogenicity and calcification — potentially boosting long-term durability and lowering manufacturing cost — though the small sample size means larger, longer-term studies are still needed.
MITRAL JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-12
Clinical Outcomes of Transfemoral Transcatheter Mitral Valve Replacement: Results From the CHOICE-MI Registry
The international CHOICE-MI registry, published in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, analyzed 124 patients (mean age 79, 50% women, median EuroSCORE II 5.0%) who underwent transfemoral transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TF TMVR) with 9 different dedicated devices across 41 centers. Technical success was 91% and procedural mortality 0.8%; 95% of patients had residual mitral regurgitation ≤1+ at discharge, largely sustained through 2 years. However, the composite of all-cause mortality or heart failure hospitalization reached 53% at 2 years (44% at 1 year), with cardiovascular mortality of 20% (15% at 1 year); atrial fibrillation independently predicted mortality/HFH (HR 3.97, p=0.022) -- "reasonable" procedural performance for TF TMVR, but a reminder of the substantial longer-term event burden in this high-risk population.
LAA JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-14
1-Year Real-World Outcomes With the Amulet Occluder From the EMERGE LAA Postapproval Study
The EMERGE LAA postapproval study, drawing on the US National Cardiovascular Data Registry (NCDR) LAAO Registry, is the largest real-world cohort of the Amulet left atrial appendage occluder to date, covering 12,180 patients with attempted implantation between August 2021 and December 2023. Implant success was 96.0% (11,693/12,180), with 88.9% success among patients with prior failed LAAO attempts, and effective closure (peridevice leak ≤3 mm) in 97.2% at 45 days. Safety outcomes included 45-day all-cause death of 0.81%, ischemic stroke 0.23%, and major bleeding 3.1%; at 1 year, all-cause death was 8.2%, any stroke 1.5% (ischemic 1.2%), and major bleeding 6.4% -- the most comprehensive real-world safety evidence for Amulet in US clinical practice to date.
TRICUSPID European Heart Journal · 2026-08-13
Combined transcatheter mitral and tricuspid edge-to-edge repair or tricuspid edge-to-edge repair alone in moderate mitral regurgitation: a propensity-matched analysis
This propensity-matched analysis of the European EuroTR registry (2016-2025) found that among 3,100 patients undergoing tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) for severe tricuspid regurgitation, 30% had untreated moderate mitral regurgitation (MR), which was associated with significantly higher 2-year mortality (37% vs 23%, p<0.0001). Among 217 propensity-matched pairs, adding concomitant mitral TEER (M-TEER) produced greater TR reduction (-1.9 vs -1.6 grades, p=0.001) and markedly better survival (87% vs 76% at 1 year; 81% vs 70% at 2 years, p=0.005) than T-TEER alone -- registry evidence supporting a combined dual-valve strategy in this subgroup.
TRICUSPID JACC · 2026-08-10
Tricuspid Valve Academic Research Consortium-2 (TVARC-2): Trial Design and Endpoints
Published in JACC, the second Tricuspid Valve Academic Research Consortium document (TVARC-2), authored by a multicenter expert panel including Weill Cornell and Duke investigators, builds on the original TVARC definitions of tricuspid regurgitation and outcomes to propose concrete trial designs and endpoint selections for three key comparison scenarios in transcatheter tricuspid valve intervention (TTVI): device versus optimal medical therapy, device versus device, and device versus surgery. The document notes that randomized evidence has already led to FDA approval and CE marking of edge-to-edge repair and replacement devices, but next-generation devices and combination strategies still lack a standardized evaluation framework — a gap TVARC-2 aims to close for sponsors and regulators alike.
MITRAL TCTMD (New York Valves 2026) · 2026-08-11
Repeat M-TEER Tied to Worse Clinical Outcomes: STS/ACC TVT Registry
At New York Valves 2026, John Saxon, MD (University of Virginia), presented an STS/ACC TVT Registry analysis of 69,139 patients who underwent mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER) between 2016 and 2023, finding that 3.8% required a second repair within 5 years. Compared with single-procedure patients, those needing a redo had higher mortality and heart failure hospitalization risk and smaller gains in health status; procedural success with repeat M-TEER was only 59%, and roughly half of redo patients developed iatrogenic mitral stenosis. The strongest predictors of needing a second repair were a single-device index procedure, final MR grade ≥2+, and a lower final mean gradient.
