Real-World Outcomes of Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Analysis From the STS/ACC TVT Registry
JAMA  ·  2026-10-16
A retrospective STS/ACC TVT Registry analysis led by Makkar and colleagues, published in JAMA, examined 1,034 patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR) treated with the EVOQUE transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) system at 82 US centers between February 2024 and March 2025 (mean age 77.1 years, 69.1% women). Procedural success exceeded 98%, and more than 97% of patients had mild or lesser TR at 30 days. Thirty-day rates were 3.1% for all-cause mortality, 0.2% for stroke, and 7.9% for severe bleeding -- bleeding and new permanent pacemaker rates both came in lower than in the pivotal TRISCEND II randomized trial (10.4% severe bleeding). The authors cautioned that the registry lacked a concurrent comparator arm and core-laboratory echocardiographic adjudication, so TTVR's long-term durability and real-world benefit still need longer follow-up.

IMAGING JAMA Cardiology · 2026-10-17
Artificial Intelligence–Enabled Acquisition and Interpretation for Screening Aortic Stenosis
A Mayo Clinic team (Lee, Naser, Kane, and colleagues) reported in JAMA Cardiology the development and validation of an AI system that both guides probe acquisition and automatically interprets focused cardiac ultrasound (FoCUS) images to screen for moderate-or-greater aortic stenosis. The algorithm was first validated on 602 exams acquired by experienced sonographers, then tested prospectively on 1,302 exams acquired by nine research staff with no prior clinical or ultrasound experience who received only four hours of training. For detecting moderate-or-greater aortic stenosis, sensitivity was 85.4%, specificity 99.7%, positive predictive value 91.1%, and negative predictive value 99.4%. A two-step workflow routing AI-positive or technically inadequate studies to expert review further improved positive predictive value while requiring expert review of only about 10% of exams. The authors frame the tool as a community screening aid rather than a diagnostic replacement, aimed at widening early detection of aortic stenosis.
TAVI JAMA Cardiology · 2026-10-18
Seven-Year Valve Durability With Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement: An Ad Hoc Analysis of the PARTNER 3 Randomized Clinical Trial
An ad hoc analysis of the PARTNER 3 randomized trial, led by Julien Ternacle and colleagues and published in JAMA Cardiology, compared 7-year valve durability between 495 low-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis who received balloon-expandable SAPIEN 3 TAVR and 453 who underwent surgical aortic valve replacement. Structural valve deterioration, endocarditis-related valve dysfunction (0.4% with TAVR vs 0.5% with surgery), and all-cause bioprosthetic valve failure requiring reintervention were low and similar between groups; freedom from all-cause valve failure among survivors at 7 years was 73% with TAVR versus 75% with surgery. Thrombosis-related valve dysfunction was more common early after TAVR but was mostly reversible and rarely progressed to failure or reintervention. An accompanying editorial called the findings reassuring evidence of durable long-term TAVR performance in low-risk patients.
TAVI JACC · 2026-10-16
10-Year Outcomes of SAPIEN 3 Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement or Surgery in Intermediate-Risk Patients
A propensity-matched analysis published in the June 16, 2026 JACC focus issue compared TAVR patients from the PARTNER 2 SAPIEN 3 Intermediate-Risk registry (P2S3i) with surgical patients from the PARTNER 2A trial: 2,005 patients (1,069 TAVR, 936 surgery) were analyzed, yielding 783 matched pairs. At 10 years, all-cause mortality was similar (83.4% TAVR vs. 82.3% surgery; HR 1.01, 95% CI 0.91-1.13, P=0.82), as was aortic valve reintervention (2.0% vs. 1.9%, P=0.47). That contrasts with a companion PARTNER 2A analysis of the earlier second-generation SAPIEN XT valve published in the same issue, where 10-year all-cause mortality was higher with TAVR (86% vs. 83% surgery; HR 1.13, P=0.02) and reintervention was also higher (6% vs. 2%, P<0.001). Together the two papers suggest that newer balloon-expandable valve design may have closed the late-term gap seen with earlier-generation transcatheter valves versus surgery, though experts caution that high background mortality among elderly patients at 10 years complicates the comparison, and durability conclusions still hinge on longer follow-up of newer valve platforms.
TAVI ACC · 2026-10-14
CMS Finalizes Updated TAVR National Coverage Determination
In September 2026, CMS finalized an updated national coverage determination (NCD) for TAVR: symptomatic severe aortic stenosis patients are now covered without the prior coverage-with-evidence-development (CED) requirement, while asymptomatic severe AS remains covered only under CMS-approved CED studies -- none of which currently exist, so that population is not yet actually covered. The policy also drops the long-standing dual-operator mandate (letting the heart team decide whether an interventional cardiologist and a cardiac surgeon must jointly perform each case), eliminates hospital TAVR volume thresholds, and removes mandatory registry participation as a coverage condition (hospitals must still run a continuous quality-improvement process). ACC, STS, and SCAI issued a joint statement welcoming the update as better matching TAVR's current role as a cornerstone therapy for severe aortic stenosis.
TAVI EHJ - Valvular and Structural Heart Disease · 2026-10-12
Challenges in the Timely Management of Severe Aortic Stenosis: A Multicentre Analysis of Delayed Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation Referrals and Gender Disparities
A multicentre observational study in the European Heart Journal - Valvular and Structural Heart Disease analyzed 519 patients (46.1% women) referred for TAVI with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis across three European centres in 2023. Women experienced substantially longer diagnostic delays from symptom onset to diagnosis than men (roughly 3 months vs 1 month), with marked variation between centres, yet once referred, time from diagnosis to TAVI and post-procedural outcomes were similar between sexes -- suggesting the sex gap in aortic stenosis care lies mainly in disease recognition and referral rather than in the TAVI pathway itself.
IMAGING JACC · 2026-10-14
Automated Alerts to Improve Timely Evaluation and Treatment of Valvular Heart Disease: The ALERT Trial
The ALERT trial, run by Medtronic and Tempus AI and published in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology (JACC) with simultaneous presentation at ACC.26, was a cluster-randomized trial spanning 5 US health systems and 35 hospitals, covering 765 clinicians who ordered 2,016 echocardiograms flagging severe aortic stenosis (AS) or mitral regurgitation (MR). Clinicians were randomized to receive an automated, AI-driven EHR alert (built on the Tempus Next platform to flag significant valve disease on echo reports) or usual care. At 90 days, the alert arm had a valve-intervention rate (including TAVR) of 13.4% versus 9.6% with usual care (a roughly 40% relative increase, P=0.005), and a multidisciplinary heart-team evaluation rate of 22.7% versus 17.9% (P=0.005); the hierarchical composite primary endpoint favored the alert arm with a win ratio of 1.27 (95% CI, 1.05-1.54, P=0.007). The authors frame ALERT as a multisite follow-on to the single-center DETECT-AS study, aimed at using automated, judgment-independent alerts to narrow persistent racial and access disparities in structural heart care -- the sponsors cite data showing roughly 90% of current US TAVR recipients are white.
TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-10-17
PCI Before TAVR May Still Be the Safest Strategy for Some Heart Patients
A SYNTAX-score subanalysis of the PRO-TAVI trial, led by Gijs M. Broeze (Amsterdam UMC) and published in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, examined 466 TAVR patients with significant coronary artery disease, comparing PCI before TAVR versus deferred PCI stratified by baseline SYNTAX score. Overall, the 1-year composite endpoint (all-cause death, MI, stroke, major bleeding) did not differ significantly across SYNTAX-score categories, and deferred PCI was consistently linked to less bleeding. However, in the highest-complexity SYNTAX subgroup, the death/MI/stroke composite was 30.9% with deferred PCI versus 14.8% with immediate PCI. The authors suggest that the extent of coronary disease should factor into PCI-timing decisions in TAVR patients with CAD, though they caution the finding comes from a subgroup analysis and needs confirmation in randomized trials.
TRICUSPID JACC: Advances · 2026-10-18
Temporal Trends in Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Interventions After FDA Approval in the United States
A US real-world analysis published in JACC: Advances by Rawlley, Gupta, and colleagues, drawing on the Vizient Clinical Data Base, examined 2,143 transcatheter tricuspid edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) procedures at 180 hospitals and 1,301 transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) procedures at 113 hospitals between October 2024 and December 2025. TTVR's share of all tricuspid interventions rose from 31.9% to 41.6% over the period, with monthly volumes of both procedures climbing steadily. In-hospital mortality was 0.5% after T-TEER versus 2.0% after TTVR; major bleeding occurred in 6.0% of T-TEER versus 12.4% of TTVR patients; and 11.3% of TTVR patients required a new permanent pacemaker. The authors note that baseline comorbidities resembled those of the pivotal trial populations, suggesting rapid real-world uptake of tricuspid interventions since FDA approval, though TTVR carries meaningfully higher periprocedural risk than T-TEER that should factor into treatment selection.
LAA Heart Rhythm · 2026-10-16
Reappraising CHAMPION-AF: Does Placebo Meet Noninferiority in Stroke Prevention?
In the CHAMPION-AF trial, presented at ACC.26 and published simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine, 3,000 patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation were randomized 1:1 to Watchman FLX left atrial appendage closure or a direct oral anticoagulant (NOAC). The 3-year composite primary endpoint (cardiovascular death, stroke, or systemic embolism) occurred in 5.7% of the device arm versus 4.8% of the NOAC arm, a 0.9-percentage-point difference (95% CI, -0.8 to 2.6) that met the prespecified 4.8-point noninferiority margin (P<0.001); on safety, non-procedural major or clinically relevant non-major bleeding was 10.9% with the device versus 19.0% with NOACs (a 45% relative reduction). A reappraisal subsequently published in Heart Rhythm, however, challenges that noninferiority conclusion: broken out on its own, the stroke endpoint showed 50 strokes in the device arm versus 33 in the NOAC arm, a hazard ratio of 1.46 (95% CI, 0.94-2.27) -- suggesting the trial's overall noninferiority result was driven largely by the bleeding advantage diluting the composite, rather than by closure genuinely matching anticoagulation on stroke prevention itself. The critique extends a long-running debate over whether left atrial appendage closure is ready to replace anticoagulation as first-line stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation.
TAVI EuroPCR 2026 · 2026-10-17
EuroPCR 2026: Coronary Revascularisation in Patients Undergoing TAVI — The ARTICA Individual Patient Data Meta-Analysis
Presented at EuroPCR 2026 (Paris, May 19-22) by Roberto Scarsini and colleagues, the ARTICA individual patient data meta-analysis pooled four randomized trials (including ACTIVATION and NOTION-3) comprising 1,050 patients with severe aortic stenosis and concomitant coronary artery disease undergoing TAVI (694 assigned to PCI -- 439 FFR-guided, 255 angiography-guided -- versus 356 to optimal medical therapy alone). At 1 year, PCI was associated with a 30% lower risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) versus medical therapy alone (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.49-0.99), driven mainly by fewer repeat revascularizations (HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.14-0.80), while net adverse clinical events including bleeding (NACE) were similar between groups. FFR-guided PCI showed benefit over medical therapy on both MACE (HR 0.58) and NACE (HR 0.68), whereas angiography-guided PCI did not. The investigators concluded that routine PCI offers only modest benefit in TAVI patients with CAD, and that a selective, physiology-guided strategy may be preferable -- a message consistent with the neutral primary results of the contemporaneous PRO-TAVI trial.
LAA ESC Congress 2026 · 2026-10-17
Left Atrial Appendage Closure During Open-Heart Surgery Only Beneficial for High-Risk Patients: The LAACS-2 Trial
The LAACS-2 trial, presented at the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Congress 2026 in Munich (August 29-September 1), was a multicenter, randomized, open-label superiority trial enrolling adults undergoing planned cardiac surgery (with or without a history of atrial fibrillation), comparing routine concomitant surgical left atrial appendage closure versus no closure. In the overall population, closure did not significantly reduce stroke, and the ESC's own press release states the results do not support routine LAA closure in all patients undergoing planned cardiac surgery. However, in a prespecified high-risk subgroup (CHA2DS2-VASc score ≥3), closure was associated with a 66% relative reduction in stroke (95% CI 0.20-0.90, P=0.01). The investigators caution that this subgroup finding, while prespecified, is hypothesis-generating rather than the primary comparison; current guidelines recommend surgical LAA closure only in patients with established AF, and follow-up continues to clarify long-term benefit.
