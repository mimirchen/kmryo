Patent Foramen Ovale Closure in Stroke and the PASCAL Classification System
JAMA Neurology  ·  2026-08-22
Investigators from the SCOPE (Systematic, Collaborative, PFO Closure Evaluation) consortium -- Saver, Kent, Kasner and colleagues -- published a secondary analysis of pooled individual patient data from six phase 3 randomized trials (n=3,740; ages 18-60 with cryptogenic stroke and patent foramen ovale) in JAMA Neurology, stratifying the benefit of transcatheter PFO closure and its atrial fibrillation (AF) risk by the PFO-Associated Stroke Causal Likelihood (PASCAL) classification. Closure produced a clear net reduction in recurrent stroke for patients classified as "probable" or "possible," far outweighing any AF risk, whereas "unlikely" patients saw no stroke-risk reduction but a markedly higher absolute excess risk of device-related AF beyond day 45 (4.41% vs 1.53% in "possible" and 0.65% in "probable"), arguing for PASCAL-guided rather than age- or diagnosis-alone patient selection for closure.

TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-20
First Large Comprehensive Core-Laboratory Evaluation of Implantation Depth and Clinical Outcomes in TAVR: Final Global Results From the Optimize PRO Prospective Study
The prospective, multicenter Optimize PRO study enrolled 603 patients with symptomatic severe aortic stenosis undergoing TAVR with self-expanding Evolut PRO/PRO+ valves, stratified by core-laboratory-adjudicated implant depth into four groups (<1mm, n=88; 1-3mm, n=196; 3-5mm, n=170; >5mm, n=149). Higher (shallower) implants were associated with significantly lower resheathing/recapture rates (27.3% vs 33.7% vs 48.8% vs 51.7%, p<0.001), shorter hospital stays, and markedly lower permanent pacemaker implantation rates (2.3% vs 9.2% vs 15.9% vs 20.3%, p<0.001), while 1-year mortality, stroke, and valve migration were similar across groups. Presented as a EuroPCR 2026 Hot Line and simultaneously published in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, this is the largest core-laboratory dataset to date linking TAVR implant depth to conduction outcomes.
TRICUSPID JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-21
Frequency and Outcomes of Acute Hemodynamic Instability Following Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Insights From the CESAR-TR-Registry
The multicenter CESAR-TR registry enrolled 200 patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation undergoing transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (mean age 78±11 years); acute hemodynamic instability (AHI) occurred in 8.5% of patients, typically within 24 hours of the procedure. In-hospital mortality was dramatically higher among patients with AHI (35.2%) versus those without (0.1%), underscoring the need for early recognition and perioperative management of the abrupt right-heart loading changes that abolishing severe TR can trigger.
LAA JACC: Clinical Electrophysiology · 2026-08-21
Early Outcomes With a Next-Generation Dual-Seal Left Atrial Appendage Occluder: Results From the VERITAS Study
The prospective, single-arm VERITAS study implanted the next-generation dual-seal Amulet 360 left atrial appendage occluder in 400 patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation (mean age 74, 38% women) across 34 global sites, achieving 99.8% implant success and complete LAA closure (no leak >3mm) in 93.9% of patients at 45 days, with 399/400 meeting the primary effectiveness endpoint. No deaths, ischemic strokes, systemic emboli, or major procedural complications occurred within 7 days or before discharge, and pericardial effusion requiring pericardiocentesis fell to 0.5% -- well below the 2.4% (AMULET-IDE) and 1.9% (EMERGE LAA) seen with prior-generation devices.
TAVI JAMA Cardiology · 2026-08-23
Treadmill Stress Test in Patients With Asymptomatic Severe Aortic Stenosis: A Prespecified Registry-Based Follow-Up of the EARLY TAVR Randomized Clinical Trial
A prespecified registry-based follow-up of the EARLY TAVR randomized trial performed standardized treadmill stress testing (TST) in 962 patients screened as asymptomatic with severe aortic stenosis across 75 US sites: 816 (84.8%) had a normal TST and were randomized to TAVR or surveillance, while 146 (15.2%) had a positive TST -- most often for exertional symptoms (50.5%), a drop in systolic blood pressure (36.2%), or ventricular arrhythmia (14.3%) -- with no deaths, cardiac arrest, syncope, or need for cardioversion during or immediately after testing. Despite a positive TST signaling need for prompt intervention, 20% of these patients remained untreated at 1 year, though 2-year mortality stayed low overall in the positive-TST group, underscoring that stress testing is safe and reliable for risk-stratifying asymptomatic severe AS even as treatment translation still lags.
TAVI JACC · 2026-08-21
Proactive Management and Treatment of Aortic Stenosis: An International Expert Perspective
An international expert perspective led by Christopher M. Cook and colleagues, published in JACC (2026;87(4):414-438), builds on the 2025 ESC/EACTS valvular heart disease guidelines challenging the traditional symptom-driven watchful-waiting paradigm. It outlines a proactive operational framework for severe aortic stenosis: early referral to a multidisciplinary Heart Valve Team, streamlined upstream evaluation, prompt aortic valve replacement for many asymptomatic patients, more urgent treatment for symptomatic patients, and structured surveillance between referring clinicians and the Heart Valve Team when intervention is deferred.
TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-21
Redo-TAVR (TAV-in-TAV) Best Practices Part 1: Short-in-Short and Short-in-Tall: A Heart and Valve Collaboratory Document
A multidisciplinary Heart and Valve Collaboratory expert panel led by Vinayak N. Bapat and Gilbert H.L. Tang reviewed bench and clinical evidence on redo-TAVR (TAV-in-TAV) for short-frame balloon-expandable valves implanted within failed short- or tall-frame prior valves ("short-in-short" and "short-in-tall"), standardizing anatomic definitions, CT-based in-vivo sizing strategies, and practical workflows for predilatation, deployment, and postdilatation -- published in the August 2026 issue of JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions to guide the growing volume of TAVR reintervention cases.
TRICUSPID JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-20
The Less the Better: Mild vs Moderate Residual Tricuspid Regurgitation After T-TEER: Insights From EuroTR
The EuroTR multicenter registry analyzed 2,545 patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation (mean age 79, 54% women) who underwent tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) at 26 European centers between 2016 and 2024, stratifying 2-year survival and survival free from heart failure hospitalization by residual TR severity (mild, moderate, severe). Worse residual TR grade was associated with progressively poorer 2-year outcomes, indicating that even an increase from mild to moderate residual TR carries clinical significance -- reinforcing that operators should aim to minimize residual TR during T-TEER. Presented as a EuroPCR 2026 Hot Line and simultaneously published in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions.
MITRAL JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-20
30-Day Outcomes of a Single-Stage Transfemoral Mitral Valve Replacement System in Severe Mitral Valve Disease
An early feasibility study of the Cephea single-stage transfemoral transseptal mitral valve replacement system enrolled 31 high-risk patients with symptomatic severe mitral valve disease (24 under the EFS protocol, 7 compassionate use; mean age 75.0±8.1 years, STS-PROM 6.4%±2.9%). At 30 days, the primary safety endpoint (survival) was met in 97% (30/31) with no procedural deaths or strokes, and the primary efficacy endpoint -- mitral regurgitation reduced to mild or less -- was achieved in 100% of patients with evaluable echocardiograms (27/27), supporting high implant success and favorable early safety in anatomically complex high-risk patients.

Boston Scientific Announces Strategic Investment in MiRus LLC
Boston Scientific announced a $1.5 billion cash investment in MiRus LLC in exchange for an approximately 33.75% non-voting equity stake, plus an exclusive option to pay an additional aggregate $3.0 billion to acquire MiRus's TAVR-Structural Heart NewCo subsidiary -- maker of the balloon-expandable SIEGEL transcatheter aortic valve -- contingent on clinical and regulatory milestones. SIEGEL's frame uses a NASA-inspired, nickel-free molybdenum-rhenium superalloy, its key differentiator from existing balloon-expandable and self-expanding valves; the deal marks Boston Scientific's third attempt to re-enter the TAVR market after discontinuing its earlier Lotus valve.
OTHER Cell Stem Cell · 2026-08-17
Human heart valve-like tissues from pluripotent stem cells with enhanced maturation recapitulate inflammatory valve disease
Researchers at Murdoch Children's Research Institute in Melbourne, led by Dr Holly Voges with collaborators from the University of Melbourne, Baker Heart and Diabetes Institute and other centers, engineered 3D human heart valve-like tissue from pluripotent stem cells that recapitulates native valve molecular maturation, published in Cell Stem Cell. Exposing the tissue to inflammatory proteins linked to rheumatic heart disease made it stiffer and reproduced biomarkers seen in diseased human valves, modeling valve inflammatory injury in a dish for the first time -- offering a new platform to study valve development/disease and screen regenerative treatments.
MITRAL Philips · 2026-08-22
FDA Clears Philips AI Solution That Provides Real-Time Guidance During Complex Minimally Invasive Heart Valve Repair
Philips received FDA 510(k) clearance for EchoNavigator R5.0 with DeviceGuide, an AI-enabled image-guidance module co-developed with Edwards Lifesciences and currently indicated for use with Edwards' PASCAL Ace mitral valve repair system. DeviceGuide automatically identifies and tracks the repair device in real time, fusing live echocardiography and fluoroscopy into a single synchronized 3D view overlaid on the operator's display to streamline device navigation during complex procedures such as transcatheter mitral edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER). The clearance followed clinical validation at US and European investigational sites, including the Structural Heart and Valve Center at NewYork-Presbyterian/Columbia University Irving Medical Center.
