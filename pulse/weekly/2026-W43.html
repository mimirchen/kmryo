As Years Pass, Mortality Grows Higher With TAVI vs SAVR in Low-Risk Patients: Meta-Analysis
TCTMD  ·  2026-10-21
A time-dependent meta-analysis presented at EACTS 2026 in Barcelona pooled 2,644 low-surgical-risk patients from the PARTNER 3, Evolut Low Risk and NOTION trials and found that while overall mortality did not differ significantly between TAVI and SAVR (HR 1.14, 95% CI 0.97-1.39), TAVI carried a significantly higher mortality risk specifically in the 5- to 7-year window (HR 1.64, 95% CI 1.27-2.12), with 7-year cumulative all-cause mortality of 26.9% for TAVI versus 23.7% for SAVR and an estimated number needed to treat of roughly 30-35 for one extra death. The finding, which largely held up across sensitivity analyses, adds to an unsettled debate over long-term survival after TAVI in low-risk, longer-life-expectancy patients.

MITRAL TCTMD · 2026-10-22
Edwards Pulls Some Lots of Sapien M3 Delivery System: FDA Alert
On October 15, 2026, the FDA issued an early alert on Edwards Lifesciences' Commander M delivery system used to implant the Sapien M3 transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR) system, after balloon bursts during inflation were linked to embolism risk and difficulty retrieving the device through the guide sheath, sometimes requiring additional surgery. As of October 5, Edwards had received three balloon-burst reports, including one death and one serious injury; four affected delivery-system lots (67718561, 67804014, 67887043 and 67887044) were flagged, with customers told to locate, remove and return them, while other lots remain unaffected and in use. The FDA noted that Sapien M3 patients are generally deemed unsuitable for surgery, making such complications especially high-risk, and said it is continuing to investigate; the alert concerns only the delivery system, with no issues identified in the Sapien M3 valve itself.
TRICUSPID TCTMD · 2026-10-24
FDA Green-Lights Pascal System for Tricuspid Valve Repair
On October 21, 2026, the FDA approved Edwards Lifesciences' PASCAL Precision system for transcatheter edge-to-edge tricuspid valve repair (T-TEER) in patients with symptomatic severe tricuspid regurgitation despite optimal medical therapy who are at intermediate-or-greater surgical risk and judged appropriate for T-TEER by a multidisciplinary heart team, based on the pivotal CLASP II TR trial; PASCAL becomes only the second FDA-approved T-TEER device after Abbott's TriClip, with one-year CLASP II TR clinical and health-status outcomes due as a late-breaker at TCT 2026 (Oct 31-Nov 3, San Diego). The same day, the FDA also cleared the PASCAL Ace system with Capture Clarity fluoroscopic markers for degenerative mitral TEER to help confirm leaflet grasping during implantation.

The Cardiovascular Research Foundation (CRF) announced on August 31, 2026 the late-breaking clinical trial and science program for TCT 2026 (San Diego, October 31–November 3), listing 28 studies to be unveiled across dedicated press briefings November 1–3, spanning coronary intervention, pharmacotherapy and structural heart disease. TAVI TCTMD · 2026-10-21
Registry Analysis Tempers Difference Between TAVI and SAVR Seen in DEDICATE-DZHK6
At EACTS 2026, Kevin Veen's team from Erasmus MC compared audited real-world SAVR patients from the Dutch national registry against the SAVR and TAVI arms of the 2024 DEDICATE-DZHK6 trial (which found TAVI noninferior to SAVR for 1-year stroke-or-death, 5.4% vs 10.0%, HR 0.53). Trial SAVR patients had significantly higher risk of the stroke-or-death composite than registry SAVR patients (HR 1.63) and of stroke alone (HR 2.96), suggesting the trial's surgical arm may have underperformed real-world surgery. However, when trial TAVI patients were compared against registry SAVR patients, TAVI's mortality benefit remained significant (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.24-0.87), highlighting how surgical-control performance can vary across centers when interpreting TAVI-vs-SAVR trials.
IMAGING Tempus AI · 2026-10-20
Tempus Receives FDA Clearance for its ECG-MR AI Product Designed to Identify Undiagnosed Mitral Regurgitation
On September 28, 2026, Tempus AI announced FDA 510(k) clearance for Tempus ECG-MR, an AI algorithm that analyzes standard 12-lead ECGs to flag patients likely to have undiagnosed moderate-or-greater mitral regurgitation. It is intended for patients 65 and older with cardiovascular risk factors, excluding those with prior mitral valve intervention or an existing MR diagnosis, is not for use on paced-rhythm ECGs or serial monitoring, and outputs a binary screening signal rather than a standalone diagnosis, meant to prompt confirmatory echocardiography. It is Tempus's fourth FDA-cleared ECG-AI product, following tools for arrhythmia, heart function and pulmonary vascular disease, underscoring the growing role of ECG-based AI screening in early detection of structural valve disease.
LAA Cardiovascular Business · 2026-10-25
Abbott Updates Heart Device Instructions to Highlight Safety Risk
On October 16, 2026, the FDA posted a Class II recall (Z-0191-2027) of Abbott's Amplatzer Amulet left atrial appendage occluder, an action Abbott initiated on September 10 covering all lots and serial numbers of eight model numbers (9-ACP2-007-016 through 9-ACP2-010-034). The action is a labeling update rather than a device defect: revised instructions for use now warn that implanting the device too close to the pulmonary artery carries an increased risk of perforation, which Abbott says could cause a serious adverse event including pericardial effusion, cardiac tamponade and potentially death. The updated IFU gives explicit distance thresholds from the landing zone to the pulmonary artery - ≤1.5 mm significant risk, 1.5-3.0 mm theoretical risk, >3.0 mm lower risk - and advises considering an alternative landing zone or device orientation where clinically appropriate. No devices are being physically recalled; clinicians are instructed to review the new IFU before future Amulet procedures.
TAVI Anteris Technologies · 2026-10-21
Anteris Advances Global PARADIGM Trial Expansion with German Regulatory Clearance
Australian medtech company Anteris Technologies announced on October 19, 2026 that Germany's Federal Institute for Drugs and Medical Devices (BfArM) has cleared patient enrollment in Germany for the global pivotal PARADIGM trial of its DurAVR transcatheter aortic valve. The randomized, All-Comers Cohort will enroll approximately 1,000 patients with severe calcific aortic stenosis, randomized 1:1 to DurAVR or a commercially available TAVR valve, with a primary endpoint of noninferiority on the composite of all-cause mortality, stroke and cardiovascular hospitalization at one year. DurAVR is marketed as the first "biomimetic" valve, designed to replicate the hemodynamic performance of a healthy native aortic valve. The company said Germany, one of Europe's largest and most established TAVR markets with an extensive network of experienced structural heart centers, should meaningfully accelerate global recruitment; the clearance authorizes trial enrollment only, not commercial marketing of the investigational valve.
TAVI SOLACI · 2026-10-22
In Vitro Comparison of Conventional BASILICA, Balloon-Assisted BASILICA, and UNICORN to Prevent Coronary Obstruction During TAV-in-TAV
SOLACI reported on October 21, 2026 on a bench (in vitro) study comparing three leaflet-modification techniques — conventional BASILICA, balloon-assisted BASILICA (BA-BASILICA), and UNICORN (radiofrequency-wire leaflet crossing with progressive balloon dilation) — for preventing coronary obstruction across short-in-tall, short-in-short, and tall-in-short TAV-in-TAV size combinations. BASILICA and BA-BASILICA produced similarly sized vertical leaflet splay areas (19.2-64.9 mm²), while UNICORN created splays 2 to 10 times larger in short-in-tall and short-in-short combinations; in tall-in-short combinations, however, intraleaflet deployment was not feasible, requiring unpredictable pre-deployment balloon laceration, and this combination carried the highest obstruction risk, up to 93%. The findings suggest leaflet-modification strategy should be individualized by TAV-in-TAV sizing rather than assuming UNICORN is universally superior to BASILICA-based techniques.
IMAGING 2 Minute Medicine · 2026-10-24
An Artificial Intelligence Model Demonstrates the Potential for Improving the Efficiency of Structural Heart Disease Screening Using Electrocardiograms
A Yale-led team published TARGET-AI in NEJM AI, a multimodal model that combines longitudinal electronic health record data with 12-lead ECGs to guide more selective, targeted deployment of AI-ECG screening for structural heart disease; trained and validated on more than 754,000 ECG-echocardiogram pairs, it discriminates 26 structural heart disease phenotypes, with an AUROC of 0.90 for left ventricular systolic dysfunction and 0.85 for severe aortic stenosis. Compared with screening every patient who receives an ECG, the model's targeted approach reduced false positives and improved the precision-sensitivity balance across both community and hospitalized cohorts, pointing to a way to cut unnecessary downstream echocardiography.
MITRAL Cardiovascular Business · 2026-10-20
Startup Behind New Heart Device Partners With Medtech Incubator
Mitria Medical — a Cleveland Clinic Innovations spinout co-invented by cardiologist Samir Kapadia and cardiac surgeon Jose Navia — announced on October 1, 2026 a strategic partnership with T45 Labs, a Silicon Valley medtech incubator, which will support product development, financing, operations and clinical/regulatory strategy; T45 founder and managing partner Marwan Berrada-Sounni becomes Mitria's new CEO. Mitria's investigational Subvalvular Spacer (SVS) is a nitinol-braid implant placed beneath the posterior mitral leaflet hinge to restore leaflet coaptation, designed for patients with functional mitral regurgitation who are not candidates for edge-to-edge clip repair. The device's first US implant was performed in March 2026 at Cleveland Clinic under the FDA's Single Patient Expanded Access (compassionate use) pathway, with six patients treated to date; it remains at an early clinical stage with no published efficacy or safety data yet.
MITRAL egnite / BioSpace · 2026-10-21
egnite's Call for Science Study on M-TEER Device Durability and Long-Term Patient Outcomes to Be Featured at TCT 2026
Cardiac imaging data company egnite announced that the winning project from its inaugural Call for Science grant program — led by Dr. John T. Saxon of UVA Health, titled "Long-Term Durability After Successful M-TEER: Insights from a Large, Real-World Imaging Database" — will be presented as Featured Clinical Research at TCT 2026 (October 31-November 3, San Diego). The study draws on data from roughly 6,000+ patients who underwent mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER) in the egnite registry, following more than 2,000 technically successful procedures for up to three years with serial echocardiograms and clinical outcomes to assess device durability and recurrent mitral regurgitation — addressing a gap Saxon says existing procedural registries such as TVT lack. The egnite registry now spans roughly 14 million patients and 6 million echocardiograms.
