Seven-Year Valve Durability With Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement: An Ad Hoc Analysis of the PARTNER 3 Randomized Clinical Trial
JAMA Cardiology  ·  2026-09-28
In this ad hoc 7-year analysis of the PARTNER 3 randomized trial by Ternacle J, Hahn RT and the PARTNER 3 investigators, published in JAMA Cardiology, the authors followed 495 TAVR (SAPIEN 3) and 453 surgical bioprosthesis recipients among the original 1,000 low-surgical-risk patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis enrolled at 71 US/Canadian centers (March 2016-October 2017). At 7 years, rates of structural valve deterioration, all-cause bioprosthetic valve failure, and reintervention remained low and statistically similar between TAVR and surgery, providing the largest randomized dataset to date supporting comparable mid-to-long-term durability of the balloon-expandable valve versus surgical bioprostheses in low-risk, younger patients.

TAVI JACC · 2026-09-30
10-Year Randomized Outcomes of Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement in Intermediate-Risk Aortic Stenosis
This 10-year re-analysis of the PARTNER 2A randomized trial, which assigned intermediate-risk aortic stenosis patients to TAVR with the second-generation balloon-expandable SAPIEN XT valve or surgery, found significantly higher all-cause mortality (86% vs 83%, HR 1.13, P=.02) and aortic valve reintervention (6% vs 2%, P<.001) with TAVR. Mortality was similar between transfemoral TAVR and surgery (84% vs 82%, P=.27), but significantly higher with transapical/transaortic TAVR versus surgery (93% vs 85%, P<.001; P for interaction=.03). A companion propensity-matched analysis using the newer third-generation SAPIEN 3 valve showed 10-year outcomes comparable to surgery, suggesting device and imaging-guided planning advances have narrowed the durability gap — but underscoring the long-term risks tied to non-transfemoral access with older-generation valves.
MITRAL JACC: Advances · 2026-10-02
Mitral Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair in Ventricular Functional Mitral Regurgitation and Very Severe Left Ventricular Dysfunction
The Japanese OCEAN-Mitral registry enrolled 1,538 patients with ventricular functional mitral regurgitation and LVEF ≤40% undergoing M-TEER, comparing those with LVEF <20% (n=103) against LVEF 20%-40% (n=1,435); acute procedural success was similarly high (100% vs 97%) and in-hospital mortality did not differ (5% vs 4%), but LVEF <20% independently predicted higher 1-year cardiovascular mortality (HR 2.00, 95% CI 1.08-3.68). Although such severely impaired patients have largely been excluded from randomized trials, this largest-to-date registry shows M-TEER remains feasible and brings meaningful symptomatic benefit without excess death or heart-failure hospitalization.
TAVI JACC: Advances · 2026-10-04
Long-Term Outcomes After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement in Nonagenarians: Impact of Frailty and Malnutrition
A Japanese multicenter registry study published in JACC: Advances (March 2026) followed 4,623 transfemoral TAVR patients, including 700 aged ≥90 years, for 5 years. All-cause mortality was significantly higher in nonagenarians (53.2% vs 37.0%, P<0.001), driven mainly by noncardiovascular deaths (32.5% vs 19.9%, P<0.001), while cardiovascular mortality was similar between groups (20.3% vs 17.0%, P=0.198). On multivariable analysis, age ≥90 itself was not an independent mortality predictor -- frailty and malnutrition were the strongest determinants of long-term outcomes, suggesting TAVR candidacy should be guided by geriatric assessment rather than chronological age alone.
TRICUSPID JACC: Asia · 2026-10-01
Asian Pacific Society of Cardiology Consensus Statements on the Use of Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair in the Treatment of Tricuspid and Mitral Regurgitation
The Asian Pacific Society of Cardiology (APSC) has issued its first regional consensus statement on transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) for tricuspid (TR) and mitral regurgitation (MR), authored by an international panel including Yeo KK, Muller DWM and Wang JA, comprising 16 recommendations. The statement calls for multidisciplinary Heart Team evaluation and rigorous imaging-based anatomic screening (leaflet mobility, annular size, coaptation) before TEER, noting that current evidence shows TEER reduces symptoms, improves quality of life and lowers heart-failure hospitalizations, offering a safe option for surgery-ineligible patients with severe TR or MR. The document addresses a gap for the Asia-Pacific region, where patient anatomy and device access can differ from Western cohorts.
TRICUSPID JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-09-28
Prognostic Value of NT-proBNP in Patients Undergoing Tricuspid Valve Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair: The EuroTR Registry
This EuroTR (European Registry of Transcatheter Repair for Tricuspid Regurgitation) analysis, published in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, included 2,282 patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation undergoing transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) with available baseline NT-proBNP (median age 80, 54% women, 86% NYHA III-IV), stratified into tertiles (≤1,674, 1,674-3,743, and >3,743 pg/mL). Higher NT-proBNP tertiles were associated with greater comorbidity burden, more advanced biventricular remodeling, and more severe TR. Baseline NT-proBNP was independently associated with the primary 2-year endpoint of all-cause mortality or first heart failure hospitalization (adjusted HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.29-2.04), and early 30-day postprocedural NT-proBNP trajectories added incremental prognostic value beyond baseline — suggesting a simple, clinically applicable risk-stratification tool for T-TEER.
MITRAL JACC: Advances · 2026-09-30
Mitral Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair in Ventricular Functional Mitral Regurgitation and Very Severe Left Ventricular Dysfunction
Drawing on Japan's multicenter OCEAN (Optimized Catheter Valvular Intervention)-Mitral registry, this study compared outcomes of mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER) in 103 patients with ventricular functional mitral regurgitation and very severe LV dysfunction (LVEF <20% — a population largely excluded from randomized M-TEER trials) versus 1,435 patients with LVEF 20%-40%. Acute procedural success was 100% in the LVEF <20% group (vs 97% in the LVEF 20%-40% group), with MR reduced to ≤1+ in 88.2% overall; in-hospital mortality was similar between groups (5% vs 4%). Both groups showed significant 1-year NYHA functional class improvement, though functional gains were more modest and cardiovascular mortality remained higher in the LVEF <20% group, likely reflecting more advanced underlying myocardial disease rather than the procedure itself. The findings provide the first substantial registry evidence supporting the feasibility and safety of M-TEER in this highest-risk population previously excluded from pivotal trials.
TRICUSPID JACC: Advances · 2026-09-30
Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Annuloplasty vs Edge-to-Edge Repair: A Propensity-Matched Multicenter Comparative Analysis
This multicenter propensity-score-matched analysis compared two distinct transcatheter strategies for severe secondary tricuspid regurgitation (TR) among 1,122 consecutive patients treated from 2017-2024 (882 with tricuspid edge-to-edge repair, T-TEER; 240 with transcatheter tricuspid valve annuloplasty, TTVA), yielding 111 well-matched pairs after 1:1 matching. At discharge, residual TR ≤1+ was achieved in 50.0% of T-TEER patients versus 34.2% of TTVA patients, and TR ≤2+ in 87.7% versus 67.6%; procedural complications were significantly less frequent with T-TEER (4.5% vs 27.0%, P<.001). One-year freedom from the composite of all-cause mortality or first heart failure hospitalization was similar between strategies (75.0% vs 79.8%, P=.408). As the largest head-to-head propensity-matched comparison to date, the study suggests T-TEER offers a more favorable safety profile and greater TR reduction, though mid-term clinical outcomes appear comparable between the two approaches.
MITRAL JACC: Advances · 2026-10-04
Sex-Specific Outcomes in Patients Undergoing Mitral Valve Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair: The REPAIR Study
The REPAIR study, published in JACC: Advances on May 13, 2026, analyzed 2,601 patients undergoing PASCAL-system transcatheter edge-to-edge mitral repair (M-TEER), including 1,150 women (44.2%) and 1,451 men (55.8%). Women achieved an "optimal result" less often than men (55.7% vs 65.9%, P<0.001), driven by a higher rate of elevated postprocedural transmitral gradients (≥5 mmHg: 24.0% vs 14.0%, P<0.001), while residual mild-or-less MR rates and technical success (97.0% vs 97.7%) were similar. One-year all-cause mortality or heart failure hospitalization did not differ significantly by sex (20.1% vs 20.8%), suggesting comparable overall safety and benefit despite smaller annular anatomy predisposing women to higher residual gradients.
MITRAL ESC Heart Failure · 2026-09-29
Five-Year Outcomes of Transcatheter Mitral Valve Replacement in Patients With Severe Symptomatic Mitral Regurgitation: Results From the Tendyne Expanded Clinical Study
This prospective, single-arm, multicentre Tendyne Expanded Clinical Study (enrolled November 2014-June 2020) followed 191 patients with severe symptomatic mitral regurgitation (88.5% secondary MR), of whom 186 (97.4%) underwent transapical Tendyne transcatheter mitral valve replacement. At 5 years, MR grade fell from ≥3+ in 99.5% of patients at baseline to ≤1+ in 95.3%; among 5-year survivors, 73.5% were in NYHA class I/II, with no structural valve deterioration observed. The results support durable, sustained elimination of MR with Tendyne TMVR in a high-surgical-risk population.
TAVI Circ: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-10-01
Mitral Regurgitation and Severe Aortic Stenosis: Clinical Characteristics, Prognostic Impact, and Response to TAVR: Insights From Egnite Data
Drawing on the Egnite real-world database of roughly 2.99 million patients and 5.25 million echocardiograms across 43 US institutions, Peterson BE and colleagues identified 36,402 patients with severe aortic stenosis (AS) and at least moderate mitral regurgitation (MR) (mean age 78.2, 54.6% male), of whom 52.0% were untreated, 36.5% underwent TAVR alone, and 11.5% had surgery or other transcatheter intervention. Two-year mortality rose with MR severity — 36.8% with less-than-moderate MR, 57.3% with moderate MR, and 65.5% with moderate-to-severe/severe MR — while 77.5% of patients treated with TAVR alone showed MR improvement, offering the largest real-world evidence yet that relieving aortic afterload alone can meaningfully reduce secondary MR and supporting a staged "TAVR-first" strategy.
