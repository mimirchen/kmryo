Short-Term Anticoagulant Therapy and Subclinical Leaflet Thickening in Transcatheter Aortic Valves: The NOTION-4 Trial
JACC  ·  2026-11-18
Published in JACC's November valvular heart disease focus issue, this randomized trial (Jørgensen, De Backer, et al.) enrolled 352 TAVR patients with no indication for long-term oral anticoagulation, randomizing them to a 3-month course of DOAC versus single antiplatelet therapy, with CT-defined hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT) as the primary endpoint. A short DOAC course significantly reduced HALT prevalence at 3 months, but this protective effect was largely attenuated by 9 months, after the DOAC had been stopped. The findings suggest short-term anticoagulation suppresses subclinical leaflet thrombosis only transiently, leaving the optimal duration of post-TAVR antithrombotic therapy unresolved.

TAVI JACC · 2026-11-18
Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement or Medical Treatment for Paradoxical Low-Flow, Low-Gradient Aortic Valve Stenosis: REBOOT-PARADOX
Published in JACC, this multicenter, open-label randomized trial led by Julinda Mehilli, Steffen Massberg and colleagues in Germany enrolled patients with symptomatic paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient severe aortic stenosis (aortic valve area ≤1.0 cm², mean gradient <40 mmHg, ejection fraction ≥50% but stroke volume index <35 mL/m²), randomizing them 2:1 to TAVR plus guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) versus GDMT alone. The trial stopped early for slow enrollment, with roughly 120 patients randomized. At 2 years, the primary endpoint of all-cause mortality occurred in 29.0% with TAVR plus GDMT versus 37.1% with GDMT alone (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.41-1.66), not statistically significant, though the TAVR group had greater symptomatic and quality-of-life improvement and fewer heart failure rehospitalizations. An accompanying editorial, "Still Searching," underscores that whether TAVI reduces mortality in this hard-to-diagnose subtype remains unresolved, keeping management of this population a point of ongoing debate.
TAVI JACC · 2026-11-22
Outcomes of Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement for Aortic Regurgitation in LVAD Patients: The JENA-VAD Study
Presented as late-breaking data at TCT 2026 and published in JACC, the prospective, multicenter, single-arm JENA-VAD registry (nested within the ALIGN-AR pivotal trial), led by Ravi Ramana and colleagues, enrolled 50 patients with severe native aortic regurgitation and a left ventricular assist device (LVAD), treated with JenaValve's dedicated self-expanding Trilogy transcatheter heart valve. Technical success and 30-day device success were each 96.0%, with no deaths or strokes at 30 days and no moderate-or-greater prosthetic regurgitation, though hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT) or valvular/aortic thrombosis occurred in 22% (11/50) of patients during follow-up. The findings offer early safety and efficacy evidence for a dedicated device in a population previously treated only via compassionate use or off-label TAVR, while full-cohort 1-year follow-up and durability data remain pending.
TAVI Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-11-18
Case Volumes and Outcomes of Early-Career Operators Performing TAVR in the United States
Published in Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions by Husam M. Salah and colleagues, this national US analysis examined TAVR procedures performed by "early-career operators" (defined as within 5 years of independent practice), who performed roughly 14.2% of TAVR cases during the study period. Despite lower cumulative case volumes and a greater share of non-elective, higher-complexity patients, early-career operators had risk-adjusted 30-day mortality (2.38% vs. 2.44%) and 30-day composite outcome rates (11.19% vs. 11.77%) similar to, or modestly better than, more experienced operators. The authors say the findings support the adequacy of current TAVR training and credentialing pathways, offering reassurance about early-career operators performing TAVR independently.
TAVI JTCVS Open / Cardiovascular Business · 2026-11-18
Heart surgery outperforms TAVR in new 5-year study of young, low-risk patients
A real-world registry analysis led by J. Hunter Mehaffey of West Virginia University tracked more than 37,000 patients treated with TAVR or surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) from 2016 to 2024, comparing 18,549 propensity-matched patients in each group. TAVR outperformed SAVR on in-hospital mortality and periprocedural stroke, but at 5 years, freedom from the composite of all-cause mortality, stroke, valve reintervention and heart failure rehospitalization was 87.5% with SAVR versus 77.8% with TAVR, with SAVR also showing lower heart failure rehospitalization and all-cause mortality. These findings diverge from 5-year randomized trial data (Evolut Low Risk, PARTNER 3) showing comparable outcomes between TAVR and surgery; as an observational registry, selection bias cannot be excluded, but the focus on younger, low-risk patients offers heart teams a real-world perspective distinct from RCT evidence, fueling ongoing debate in the structural heart community over TAVR's expanding use in younger patients.
