TAVR in Patients with Moderate Aortic Stenosis with At-Risk Features: Primary Results of the PROGRESS Trial
NEJM / TCT 2026  ·  2026-11-03
At TCT 2026 in San Diego on November 2, 2026, Philippe Genereux, MD, presented primary results of the randomized PROGRESS trial, published simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine. Between October 2021 and January 2024, 744 patients with moderate aortic stenosis and at least one at-risk feature were randomized 1:1 to transfemoral balloon-expandable SAPIEN 3 TAVR or guideline-directed clinical surveillance. The composite primary endpoint of death or a heart-failure event at 2 years occurred in 16.7% of the TAVR group versus 23.9% of the surveillance group, a 33% relative risk reduction; 57.4% of surveillance patients crossed over to TAVR within 2 years, with no meaningful difference in procedure-related adverse events between arms.

LAA JAMA / TCT 2026 · 2026-11-03
SIMPLAAFY Study of the WATCHMAN FLX Pro Left Atrial Appendage Closure Device Meets Primary Endpoints
On November 2, 2026, Boston Scientific presented primary results of the randomized SIMPLAAFY trial at TCT 2026, published simultaneously in JAMA. The study randomized 1,857 atrial fibrillation patients across 86 U.S. sites who received a WATCHMAN FLX Pro left atrial appendage closure device to post-implant aspirin monotherapy, reduced-dose NOAC (apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily or rivaroxaban 10 mg daily for 3 months), or dual antiplatelet therapy. Both monotherapy regimens met non-inferiority versus DAPT for the composite primary endpoint of death, stroke, systemic embolism and major bleeding at 6 months, with the reduced-dose NOAC arm showing a lower rate of device-related thrombosis than DAPT, supporting simplified post-procedure antithrombotic regimens after LAA closure.
LAA JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions / TCT 2026 · 2026-11-04
VERITAS Trial: One-Year Outcomes of Left Atrial Appendage Closure With the New Amulet 360 Device
The VERITAS trial of Abbott's next-generation Amplatzer Amulet 360 left atrial appendage occluder, presented at TCT 2026 on November 1 and published simultaneously in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, enrolled 458 patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation. At one year, complete LAA closure on transesophageal echo was 96.3% (95% CI 93.2-98.2%), ischemic stroke or systemic embolism occurred in 1.8%, clinically relevant device-related thrombosis in 0.4%, with no device embolization; implant success was 99.3% and mean procedure time 37 minutes. The device remains investigational in the US pending FDA approval.
MITRAL JACC / TCT 2026 · 2026-11-08
ENCIRCLE Trial: 2-Year Outcomes of Transfemoral Transcatheter Mitral Valve Replacement With SAPIEN M3
Two-year outcomes from the ENCIRCLE trial, presented as a TCT 2026 late-breaker and published simultaneously in JACC, show that among 299 patients with severe symptomatic mitral regurgitation ineligible for surgery or TEER who received Edwards' transseptal SAPIEN M3 valve, 202 completed 2-year follow-up. The composite of all-cause death or heart-failure hospitalization was 35.6% at 2 years (up from 25.2% at 1 year, which had beaten the 45% performance goal, p<0.0001), with freedom from death and from HF hospitalization at roughly 75% and 77%, respectively. An accompanying editorial by Vora and Tang notes durable MR elimination through 2 years, with most device-related risk clustering in year one, while longer-term and randomized data are still needed.
TAVI TCT 2026 / JACC · 2026-11-06
SINGLE-CLOSURE: Single- vs Double-ProGlide Vascular Closure After Transfemoral TAVR
Presented at TCT 2026 in San Diego (Nov 2) and published simultaneously in JACC, the SINGLE-CLOSURE trial randomized 876 patients (age ≥65, 45% women) undergoing transfemoral TAVR at 25 Chinese centers 1:1 to single- vs double-ProGlide vascular closure. The in-hospital composite of VARC-3 access-site complications (primary endpoint) occurred in 13.1% (57/436) of the single-ProGlide group, mostly minor; major complications were rare in both arms (0.5% vs 1.4%), 30-day mortality was similar (2.1% vs 2.3%), and access-site stenosis was numerically lower with single-ProGlide (5.0% vs 7.9%), though bailout closure devices were used more often (2.5% vs 0.9%). The findings support a simplified single-device closure strategy after transfemoral TAVR.
OTHER TCT 2026 / JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-11-06
PARADIGM 30-Day Results: Amplatzer Valvular Plug III Shows High Procedural Success for Paravalvular Leak Closure
Following the FDA's Oct. 28 approval of Abbott's Amplatzer Paraclose (Amplatzer Valvular Plug III) as the first US transcatheter paravalvular leak (PVL) closure device, 30-day outcomes from the supporting PARADIGM study (~200 patients in the US, Europe and Canada with PVL after surgical aortic or mitral valve replacement) were presented at TCT 2026 and published simultaneously in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions. Heart failure was the indication in nearly 90% of patients and 65% had mitral PVL; device implantation succeeded in 96% with no procedure-related deaths, and roughly 77% achieved 30-day freedom from unplanned reintervention. Lead investigator Ignacio Cruz-Gonzalez said the trial met its primary endpoint with favorable short-term outcomes.
TRICUSPID Edwards Lifesciences / TCT 2026 · 2026-11-03
CLASP II TR Trial Demonstrates Superior Clinical and Quality-of-Life Benefits With Edwards PASCAL System
On November 2, 2026, Edwards Lifesciences reported primary results of the randomized CLASP II TR trial at TCT 2026, comparing its PASCAL transcatheter tricuspid valve repair system against medical therapy alone in patients with severe, symptomatic tricuspid regurgitation. PASCAL met its hierarchical composite primary endpoint, spanning mortality, durable RVAD implantation or transplant, tricuspid reintervention, heart-failure hospitalization and quality of life. At one year, 80% of PASCAL-treated patients had TR of moderate severity or less versus 30% of controls, with an 18-point improvement in KCCQ quality-of-life score in the device arm. The PASCAL Precision system received FDA approval for a defined severe-TR indication on October 21, 2026.
TRICUSPID TCT 2026 · 2026-11-04
TRAVEL III: Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement With the LuX-Valve Plus System vs. Medical Therapy for Severe Tricuspid Regurgitation
Presented at TCT 2026 on November 1, the China-based randomized TRAVEL III trial (led by Junbo Ge) assigned 126 surgical high-risk patients with severe or greater tricuspid regurgitation and NYHA III-IV symptoms 1:1 to transjugular transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) with the LuX-Valve Plus system plus optimal medical therapy (OMT), or OMT alone. The composite 6-month efficacy endpoint (survival, freedom from HF rehospitalization, TR reduction of at least one grade, and NYHA class improvement of at least one class) was met in 82.9% of TTVR+OMT patients versus 4.8% with OMT alone (rate difference 78.1%, 95% CI 67.5-88.8; p<0.001); implant success was 98.7% with a mean procedure time of 148 minutes, providing the first randomized evidence for the LuX-Valve Plus system.
MITRAL TCT 2026 / TCTMD · 2026-11-05
Global Burden of Degenerative Mitral Valve Disease Continues to Rise
At TCT 2026 in San Diego on November 2, Juan Granada presented a Global Burden of Disease analysis of degenerative mitral valve disease showing global prevalence reached 16.1 million in 2023, roughly double the 1990 figure. Age-standardized prevalence was 2.1 times higher in men, yet women accounted for 1.6 times more deaths in 2023, pointing to delayed diagnosis and undertreatment among women. Granada said the disease is systematically underdiagnosed in resource-limited regions and called for greater screening and earlier referral.
TAVI TCT 2026 / TCTMD · 2026-11-05
EARLY TAVR: Economic Analysis Supports TAVI Over Clinical Surveillance for Asymptomatic AS
A health-economic analysis of the EARLY TAVR trial, presented November 1 at TCT 2026 in San Diego, found that early transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) in asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis added about $4,500 in lifetime cost and 0.3 QALYs versus guideline-directed clinical surveillance, yielding an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) of $14,698 per QALY gained and a 91.3% probability of being cost-effective from a system perspective at the commonly used $120,000/QALY threshold; a sensitivity analysis incorporating repeat valve intervention costs at 10 years still showed early TAVR cost-effective at roughly $25,000 per QALY. The findings add economic support for earlier intervention in asymptomatic severe AS.
TAVI TCT 2026 / ACC · 2026-11-06
PROTEMBO IDE Trial: Cerebral Embolic Protection During TAVR Misses Efficacy Endpoint Despite Meeting Safety Endpoint
Presented on the final day of TCT 2026 (Nov 3, San Diego), the pivotal PROTEMBO IDE trial randomized 305 TAVR patients 2:1:1 to the ProtEmbo cerebral protection device, the Sentinel device, or no cerebral embolic protection, with the primary imaging endpoint being total new lesion volume on MRI at 36 hours, followed to 30 days. The trial missed its primary efficacy endpoint but met its primary safety endpoint; reported 30-day MACCE rates were 8.5% with Sentinel and 11.6% with no protection (the ProtEmbo arm's figure was not disclosed). The mixed result adds uncertainty about routine use of cerebral embolic protection during TAVR and became a talking point among attendees.
TRICUSPID TCT 2026 · 2026-11-04
TCT 2026: Severity-Based Subgroup Results From the TRIC-I-HF-DZHK24 Trial of Transcatheter Tricuspid Repair in Heart Failure
TRIC-I-HF-DZHK24, a German multicenter randomized trial of 369 patients with at least severe tricuspid regurgitation and elevated heart-failure risk, reported its primary results at the ESC Congress in August 2026, showing that transcatheter tricuspid repair plus medical therapy significantly cut the composite of all-cause death or HF hospitalization versus medical therapy alone. At TCT 2026 on November 1, Jörg Hausleiter presented a severity-based subgroup analysis (baseline TR severe in 50.8%, massive in 34.2%, torrential in 10.3%): the severe and massive subgroups tracked the overall benefit, but the smaller torrential-TR subgroup did not yet show conclusive benefit, highlighting a gap in the evidence for this group.
