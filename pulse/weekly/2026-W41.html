Outcomes of Patients With Reduced Left Ventricular Function Undergoing Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion
JACC: Clinical Electrophysiology  ·  2026-10-07
A large NCDR LAAO Registry analysis identified 186,085 patients undergoing left atrial appendage occlusion with documented LVEF between December 2015 and December 2023, including 5,192 (2.8%) with LVEF <30%. Procedural success (98.0% vs 98.1%, P=0.12) and in-hospital mortality (adjusted OR 1.39, 95% CI 0.76-2.55) were similar between patients with LVEF <30% and LVEF ≥50%, and 1-year stroke rates did not differ significantly. However, all-cause mortality at 1 year rose significantly with every 10% drop in LVEF, with the highest risk in the LVEF <30% group (adjusted HR 1.93, 95% CI 1.75-2.12), suggesting severely reduced LV function should not by itself preclude LAAO despite its link to higher longer-term mortality.

TAVI JACC: Advances · 2026-10-08
Antiplatelet Therapy and All-Cause Mortality After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement: The TRITAVI Registry
The multicenter TRITAVI registry analyzed 10,071 patients undergoing TAVR, with a focused comparison of single (SAPT) versus dual (DAPT) antiplatelet therapy in a femoral-access subgroup (a SCAI-presented subset included 5,514 patients: 3,197 on SAPT, 2,317 on DAPT). SAPT was associated with lower 6-month all-cause mortality (2.4% vs 5.4% with DAPT) and less major bleeding (0.5% vs 1.3%); after adjustment, DAPT carried a significantly higher 6-month mortality risk (HR 1.69, 95% CI 1.20-2.37) that persisted at 24 months (HR 1.21, 95% CI 1.01-1.46). The findings suggest single antiplatelet therapy may lower both death and bleeding risk compared with dual therapy in TAVR patients without an indication for long-term anticoagulation, though the authors caution this is observational registry evidence requiring prospective randomized confirmation.
TRICUSPID JACC: Advances · 2026-10-08
Temporal Trends in Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Interventions After FDA Approval in the United States
A U.S. national cohort analysis tracked adoption trends in transcatheter tricuspid edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) and transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) following FDA approval of tricuspid devices. Monthly T-TEER volumes rose from 128 cases in October 2024 to 156 in December 2025, while TTVR volumes grew from 60 to 111 over the same period. In-hospital mortality was lower with T-TEER (0.5%) than TTVR (2.0%), and in-hospital major bleeding was likewise lower (6.0% vs 12.4%), offering real-world evidence to inform procedure selection as tricuspid device use expands.
TAVI EuroIntervention · 2026-10-09
Long-Term Structural Valve Deterioration After TAVI: Insights From the EORP-ESC Valve Durability TAVI Registry
The EORP-ESC Valve Durability TAVI Registry, coordinated across 22 European centres under the ESC's EurObservational Research Programme, followed 597 surviving TAVI patients (mean age 79.6±7.1 years at implantation, 47.2% male) with echocardiography within 6 months of enrollment and again at 5+ years, grading structural valve deterioration (SVD) by VARC-3 criteria. At a median follow-up of 6.1 years (IQR 5.2-7.3), the crude incidence of moderate/severe SVD was 9.5% (n=57; moderate 6.2%, severe 3.4%). Multivariable analysis identified three independent predictors: intra-annular valve design (HR 38.44), small valve size (HR 4.82), and moderate-to-severe paravalvular leak (HR 3.64). The authors say the findings argue for caution when choosing older intra-annular designs in younger TAVI candidates, while cautioning that this is observational registry data with only 61% of echocardiograms read by a core laboratory.
MITRAL Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-10-08
Single TriClip Steerable Guide for Combined MitraClip and TriClip Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair (STriC-TEER): A Multicenter Experience
A multicenter retrospective study from Hong Kong, Taiwan, Thailand, and Australia reports STriC-TEER, a technique using a single TriClip steerable guide catheter (SGC) to deliver both the MitraClip system for mitral edge-to-edge repair and, after withdrawing the same guide into the right atrium, the TriClip system for tricuspid repair -- avoiding the catheter exchange required in conventional combined procedures. The series included 40 patients treated between 2023 and 2025 (mean age 76.8±9.1 years, 70% male, 97.1% with atrial fibrillation), four of whom also underwent concurrent left atrial appendage occlusion. The authors call the single-guide strategy safe and feasible, while noting that prospective randomized trials are needed to compare it with the conventional catheter-exchange approach for efficiency, outcomes, and cost-effectiveness.
TAVI JACC: Advances · 2026-10-06
Safety of Selective and Routine Femoral Access Angiography Post-Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation
A retrospective two-site analysis in JACC: Advances (September 2026) compared outcomes in 1,147 patients undergoing transfemoral TAVI under two post-procedural strategies: routine iliofemoral angiography (n=674, Bad Krozingen) versus selective, clinically-driven angiography (n=473, Freiburg). Overall VARC-3 vascular/access-site complications were similar (selective 6.6% vs routine 8.0%; OR 0.76, 95% CI 0.48-1.19), but routine angiography detected more access-site bleeding events (13.9% vs 5.2%, P<0.001) with no difference in hemoglobin drop, transfusion, or bleeding-related death; vascular reinterventions were comparable overall (4.2% vs 6.5%, P=0.095), with routine angiography favoring catheter-based fixes and selective angiography more often needing surgical or thrombin-based repair. The findings suggest selective, clinically-driven angiography may be a reasonable and less resource-intensive default strategy after transfemoral TAVI.
TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-10-07
Redo TAVR With Self-Expanding Valves Linked to Positive Early Outcomes
An analysis of the STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry examined more than 400 patients who underwent redo-TAVR for failed prior TAVR valves, all receiving supra-annular, self-expanding Medtronic devices (Evolut R, Evolut PRO, and Evolut PRO+). After adjustment for relevant clinical covariates, redo-TAVR was associated with 30-day and 1-year mortality and stroke rates comparable to native TAVR, with gains in functional status and quality of life sustained through 1 year, supporting self-expanding redo-TAVR as a viable lifetime-management strategy after valve failure.
TRICUSPID JACC: Advances · 2026-10-10
Temporal Trends in Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Interventions After FDA Approval in the United States
A retrospective cohort study in JACC: Advances used the Vizient Clinical Data Base to track early commercial adoption of transcatheter tricuspid valve interventions in the US between October 2024 and December 2025, following their FDA approval. The analysis included 2,143 transcatheter tricuspid edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) procedures at 180 hospitals and 1,301 transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) procedures at 113 hospitals, with monthly volumes for both rising steadily and TTVR's share of all tricuspid interventions growing from about 32% to about 42%. In-hospital mortality was 0.5% after T-TEER versus 2.0% after TTVR; major bleeding occurred in 6.0% and 12.4%, respectively; and 11.3% of TTVR patients required a new permanent pacemaker. Baseline comorbidities resembled those of the pivotal trial populations. The authors conclude that early real-world commercial experience shows rapid uptake of both procedures with generally favorable outcomes, though TTVR's bleeding and pacemaker burden warrant continued attention.
MITRAL Structural Heart · 2026-10-05
Sex Disparities in In-Hospital Outcomes After Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Mitral Valve Repair: A Nationwide Inpatient Sample Analysis, 2016-2022
A Nationwide Inpatient Sample analysis (2016-2022) published in Structural Heart in October 2026 examined sex-specific in-hospital outcomes after transcatheter edge-to-edge mitral valve repair (M-TEER). Women had significantly higher rates of in-hospital bleeding and cardiac tamponade than men, but in-hospital mortality did not differ significantly between sexes (1.98% in women vs 1.75% in men after multivariable adjustment); rates of stroke, myocardial infarction, and other major complications were similar between groups. The findings suggest that although women face a higher burden of periprocedural bleeding and tamponade -- likely related to smaller body size and anatomy -- their overall in-hospital survival after M-TEER is comparable to men's, offering large real-world evidence for sex-specific risk counseling.
MITRAL Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-10-11
Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair in Older Adults With Mitral and Tricuspid Valve Disease
A review published online September 11, 2026 in Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions notes that as populations age, a growing number of older adults with significant mitral or tricuspid regurgitation are poor surgical candidates because of frailty and multiple comorbidities. Drawing on randomized trial and registry evidence, the authors conclude that transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) provides durable regurgitation reduction, functional improvement, and fewer heart-failure hospitalizations with acceptable safety across age groups, but argue that geriatric assessment tools for frailty, cognition, nutrition, and polypharmacy should be incorporated routinely to better select candidates and optimize periprocedural care.
TRICUSPID Annals of Cardiothoracic Surgery · 2026-10-05
Features and short-term outcomes of real-world transcatheter tricuspid valve repair vs. replacement in Asia-Pacific
A retrospective multicenter study from four centers in Hong Kong, Taiwan, and Thailand, published in Annals of Cardiothoracic Surgery in March 2026, analyzed 174 patients with severe symptomatic tricuspid regurgitation (TR) treated between 2017 and 2025: 136 underwent tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) and 38 underwent transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR, orthotopic or heterotopic). At 30 days, TR reduction to moderate or less was achieved in 100.0% of TTVR patients versus 74.0% of T-TEER patients (P=0.001), while NYHA class I/II improvement was similar between groups (93.7% vs 96.2%). However, TTVR was associated with significantly higher in-hospital major adverse events (15.8% vs 2.2%, P=0.003), longer hospital stays (median 15 vs 5 days, P<0.001), and a greater platelet count decline. The findings suggest that in the Asia-Pacific region, TTVR is currently reserved mainly for patients with anatomy unsuitable for T-TEER, and while both approaches improve TR and symptoms, TTVR carries higher procedural risk.
IMAGING European Heart Journal - Digital Health · 2026-10-06
Validation and Longitudinal Trajectory Analysis of an AI-Based ECG Model for Aortic Stenosis: From Community Screening to Pre-TAVR Risk Stratification
A validation study published in European Heart Journal - Digital Health (2026) evaluated AccurKardia's AI-enabled ECG model AK-AVS for aortic stenosis (AS), testing it in both a community-based screening population and in TAVR patients at Baylor St. Luke's Medical Center. The AI-ECG model detected AS-related signal up to 4.5 years before TAVR, and patients with a positive AI-ECG but a negative echocardiogram had a 4.4-fold higher risk of future AS-related hospitalization over a median 6.2-year follow-up. Unsupervised clustering of longitudinal AI-ECG trajectories in TAVR patients also independently predicted 1-year post-TAVR mortality beyond established risk scores such as the STS score and EuroSCORE, suggesting AI-ECG could push AS screening upstream of symptomatic, echo-confirmed disease while adding prognostic information after TAVR.
