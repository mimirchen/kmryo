Left Atrial Appendage Closure or Anticoagulation for Atrial Fibrillation
NEJM  ·  2026-08-08
The NEJM-published CHAMPION-AF randomized trial, presented at ACC 2026, reported 3-year outcomes in 3,000 atrial fibrillation patients eligible for oral anticoagulation (mean age 71.7, 31.9% women) randomized to Watchman FLX left atrial appendage closure (Boston Scientific) versus a NOAC. The composite of cardiovascular death, stroke, or systemic embolism occurred in 5.7% of the LAAC group versus 4.8% with NOACs, meeting the prespecified non-inferiority margin; annualized ischemic stroke/systemic embolism was 1.1%, and ISTH bleeding (including procedural) was reduced by 34% (12.8% vs 19.0% at 36 months). The trial is the first randomized evidence supporting LAAC as a potential first-line stroke-prevention option, though industry funding and lower-than-expected event rates have fueled debate over the strength of its non-inferiority claim.

TRICUSPID ESC Congress 2026 / NEJM · 2026-08-31
Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Repair Reduces Death and Heart Failure Hospitalisation in Severe Tricuspid Regurgitation: The TRIC-I-HF Trial
Presented in a Hot Line session at ESC Congress 2026 on August 30, 2026 and published simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine, the investigator-initiated TRIC-I-HF-DZHK24 trial, conducted at 29 high-volume heart-valve centres in Germany, randomized 360 patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation and manifest right-sided heart failure (mean age 80.3, 56.4% women, 75% NYHA III/IV) 2:1 to transcatheter tricuspid valve intervention (TTVI, using CE-marked PASCAL/PASCAL ACE, TriClip, or Cardioband devices) plus optimal medical therapy (n=237) versus optimal medical therapy alone (n=123). Two hierarchically tested co-primary endpoints were used: a win-ratio composite of death, heart-failure hospitalisation, and quality of life at 1 year, and — tested only if the first reached significance — a composite of all-cause death or heart-failure hospitalisation at 3 years, for which event-free survival was significantly higher with TTVI (52.4% vs 21.0%; HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.29–0.55; P<0.001). This is the first randomized trial to show that transcatheter tricuspid repair reduces hard mortality/hospitalisation endpoints, filling a long-standing evidence gap in the field.
LAA NEJM · 2026-08-10
Left Atrial Appendage Closure or Medical Therapy in Atrial Fibrillation
Published in NEJM, the CLOSURE-AF trial (Landmesser et al) randomized 912 atrial fibrillation patients at high risk of both stroke (mean CHA2DS2-VASc 5.2) and bleeding (mean HAS-BLED 3.0) across 42 German centers to left atrial appendage closure (n=446) or physician-directed intensified medical therapy including NOACs (n=442). Over a median 3-year follow-up, the intention-to-treat annualized rate of the primary composite endpoint (stroke, systemic embolism, cardiovascular/unexplained death, or major bleeding) was 16.83 per 100 patient-years with LAAC versus 13.27 with medical therapy (adjusted HR 1.28, 95% CI 1.01-1.62), missing the prespecified noninferiority margin of 1.3. The negative result stands in sharp contrast to the nearly concurrent CHAMPION-AF trial, underscoring how patient selection — especially in older, multimorbid populations — may determine whether LAAC outperforms medical therapy, and has already prompted calls for cautious interpretation in the European Heart Journal.
LAA NEJM · 2026-08-24
Left Atrial Appendage Closure or Anticoagulation for Atrial Fibrillation
CHAMPION-AF, the largest randomized trial testing left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) as a first-line stroke-prevention strategy, enrolled 3,000 patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation at 141 sites across the US, Canada, Europe, Japan, and Australia, randomizing them to the Watchman FLX device or a NOAC. At 3 years, LAAC was noninferior to NOACs for the primary efficacy endpoint of cardiovascular death, stroke, or systemic embolism (5.7% vs 4.8%) and superior for non-procedure-related major/clinically relevant bleeding (10.9% vs 19.0%, a 45% relative reduction, P<0.001), with a favorable net clinical benefit (15.1% vs 21.8%, P<0.001). Published simultaneously in NEJM, the results support LAAC as a reasonable first-line alternative to anticoagulation even in patients who can tolerate NOACs, though some experts caution about weighing device-related complications against the long-term benefit.
TAVI ESC Congress 2026 / NEJM · 2026-08-31
TAVI-First Strategy Noninferior to PCI-First in Patients With Aortic Stenosis and Coronary Artery Disease: The TAVI-PCI Trial
This randomized controlled trial (TAVI-PCI), presented in a Hot Line session at ESC Congress 2026 on August 30, 2026 and published simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine, enrolled 986 patients with severe aortic stenosis and concomitant coronary artery disease across 48 centres in Austria, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, and Switzerland (mean age 82, 34% women). Patients were randomized 1:1 to TAVI followed by angiography-guided PCI, or PCI followed by TAVI, with both procedures completed within 1–45 days of each other. The primary endpoint — a composite of all-cause death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, ischaemia-driven revascularisation, valve-, procedure- or heart-failure-related rehospitalisation, or life-threatening, disabling or major bleeding at 1 year — occurred in 22.2% of the TAVI-first group versus 24.2% of the PCI-first group (risk difference −2.0 percentage points; 95% CI −7.4 to 3.4; P<0.001 for noninferiority), with no evidence of heterogeneity across prespecified subgroups including age, sex, and PCI complexity. The findings suggest PCI does not have to precede valve replacement in patients requiring both procedures, offering more flexibility in procedural scheduling.
TRICUSPID JAMA · 2026-08-07
Real-World Outcomes of Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Analysis From the STS/ACC TVT Registry
A JAMA-published analysis of the STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry evaluated 1,034 consecutive patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation who underwent transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR, all Evoque devices) at 82 US centers between February 2024 and March 2025 (mean age 77, 69% female, 11% Black). Valve implantation succeeded in over 98% of cases, TR was reduced to mild or less in nearly all patients by 30 days, and mortality, stroke, and bleeding outcomes were consistent with the TRISCEND II pivotal trial - with lower rates of new cardiac device implantation and bleeding than in the randomized trial - providing the first large-scale US real-world confirmation of safety and effectiveness since the device's 2024 FDA approval.
OTHER JAMA Neurology · 2026-08-22
Patent Foramen Ovale Closure in Stroke and the PASCAL Classification System
Investigators from the SCOPE (Systematic, Collaborative, PFO Closure Evaluation) consortium -- Saver, Kent, Kasner and colleagues -- published a secondary analysis of pooled individual patient data from six phase 3 randomized trials (n=3,740; ages 18-60 with cryptogenic stroke and patent foramen ovale) in JAMA Neurology, stratifying the benefit of transcatheter PFO closure and its atrial fibrillation (AF) risk by the PFO-Associated Stroke Causal Likelihood (PASCAL) classification. Closure produced a clear net reduction in recurrent stroke for patients classified as "probable" or "possible," far outweighing any AF risk, whereas "unlikely" patients saw no stroke-risk reduction but a markedly higher absolute excess risk of device-related AF beyond day 45 (4.41% vs 1.53% in "possible" and 0.65% in "probable"), arguing for PASCAL-guided rather than age- or diagnosis-alone patient selection for closure.
TAVI Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-09
Outcomes of Patients With New Left Bundle Branch Block After TAVR: TVT Registry Insights
An analysis of the STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry covering 202,533 TAVR recipients discharged alive between 2016 and 2022 found that 16.3% (32,933 patients) developed new left bundle branch block (LBBB), with incidence falling from 19.9% in Q1 2016 to 14.4% in Q3 2022. New LBBB was associated with a markedly higher rate of post-discharge permanent pacemaker implantation (7.1% vs 2.1%; adjusted HR 3.50; P<0.001), plus increased all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, rehospitalization, and worsening left ventricular systolic function, underscoring the need for closer rhythm monitoring in these patients.
TAVI Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-25
Balloon- Versus Self-Expanding Transcatheter Valves for Failed Small Surgical Aortic Bioprostheses: 3-Year Results of the LYTEN Trial
The randomized LYTEN trial compared balloon-expandable (SAPIEN 3/ULTRA, n=46) and self-expanding (Evolut R/PRO/PRO+, n=52) transcatheter valves for valve-in-valve TAVR in 98 patients with failed small (≤23mm) surgical aortic bioprostheses. At 3 years, a mean gradient ≥20 mmHg occurred in only 17.6% of self-expanding-valve patients versus 62.1% of balloon-expandable-valve patients (P<0.001), and intended valve performance was met in 82.4% versus 27.6% (P<0.001), with similar clinical outcomes between groups. Published in Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions, these are the longest randomized follow-up data yet on device selection for small failed surgical valves, showing the self-expanding valve's hemodynamic advantage persists through 3 years.
TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-01
Hospital Volume and Failure to Rescue Post-TAVR: Insights From the STS/ACC TVT Registry
Using the STS/ACC TVT Registry, researchers analyzed 487,159 TAVR patients (median age 79, 57% male) across 808 US sites from 2017-2023, stratifying major complications and failure to rescue (30-day mortality after a major complication) by annualized hospital volume. Lower-volume sites had significantly higher 30-day mortality (adjusted OR 1.12, 95% CI 1.00-1.25, p=0.045) and major complications (adjusted OR 1.19, 95% CI 1.08-1.32, p=0.0006), but failure-to-rescue rates did not differ significantly by volume (adjusted OR 0.97 per 100 cases, 95% CI 0.93-1.01, p=0.11); the overall failure-to-rescue rate among complicated patients was 11%. The findings suggest the volume-mortality relationship in TAVR is driven mainly by higher complication rates at low-volume centers rather than differences in rescue capability.
TRICUSPID JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-08-05
The Less the Better: Mild vs Moderate Residual Tricuspid Regurgitation After T-TEER: Insights From EuroTR
The multicenter European EuroTR registry followed 2,545 patients (mean age 79, 54% women) who underwent tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) at 26 centers from 2016-2024 (baseline TR severe in 44%, massive in 33%, torrential in 20%). Even a single step-up in residual TR - from none/trace to mild, or mild to moderate - was linked to significantly lower 2-year survival and survival free from heart failure hospitalization, arguing that procedural success should target near-none residual TR rather than the conventional "mild or less" threshold.
TAVI JACC · 2026-08-08
Six-Year Outcomes After Transcatheter vs Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement in Low-Risk Patients With Aortic Stenosis
Six-year outcomes from the randomized Evolut Low Risk trial, published in JACC, followed 1,414 low-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis enrolled in 2016-2019 comparing self-expanding TAVR (Medtronic Evolut) with surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR). The primary endpoint of all-cause mortality or disabling stroke showed no significant difference through 6 years, though the survival curves crossed at 5 years to numerically favor surgery. Reintervention was 5.5% with TAVR versus 3.3% with SAVR at 6 years (p=0.07), widening to a statistically significant 9.8% versus 6.0% when follow-up extended to 7 years - driven mainly by bioprosthetic aortic regurgitation rather than valve stenosis (5.6% vs 1.6%), a signal that longer-term reintervention risk should factor into TAVR decisions for younger, low-risk patients.
TAVI JACC · 2026-08-11
10-Year Outcomes of SAPIEN 3 Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement or Surgery in Intermediate-Risk Patients
Published in JACC (2026;87:3296-3308), this propensity-score-matched analysis compared 783 intermediate-surgical-risk patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis who received SAPIEN 3 TAVR in the PARTNER 2 SAPIEN 3 Intermediate-Risk Registry (P2S3i) against 783 matched patients who underwent SAVR in the PARTNER 2A randomized trial (mean age 82, 43% women, mean STS score 5.5%). At 10 years, TAVR and surgery showed similar rates of mortality and aortic valve reintervention, with comparable hemodynamic stability — among the longest follow-up data yet for SAPIEN 3 durability — though the authors note differential loss to follow-up and competing mortality risk in this elderly population complicate interpretation of such extended-term data.
TAVI JACC · 2026-08-12
Health Status Outcomes 7 Years After Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement in Low-Surgical Risk Patients With Aortic Stenosis
Published in JACC, this 7-year health-status analysis of the PARTNER 3 randomized trial (943 low-surgical-risk patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis; 494 TAVR, 449 SAVR) using the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ-OS) found TAVR conferred substantially higher scores at 1 month (mean difference +16.2 points) and modestly higher scores through 2 years (+1.9 points), with no significant between-group difference from years 3 through 7. At 7 years, roughly 60% of patients in both arms achieved an "excellent outcome" (alive, KCCQ-OS ≥75, no ≥10-point decline from baseline) -- confirming faster early recovery with TAVR but comparable long-term patient-reported health status versus surgery.
OTHER JACC · 2026-08-14
Population Prevalence of Valvular Heart Disease in the United States: The PREVUE-VALVE Study
The PREVUE-VALVE study used a decentralized, representative sampling approach -- randomly recruiting 3,000 US adults aged 65-85 via electronic invitation and mail for standardized echocardiographic screening -- to determine the true population prevalence of valvular heart disease (VHD). Moderate-or-greater VHD was found in 8.2% of participants, rising to 18.4% when mild-moderate disease was included; tricuspid regurgitation was most common (3.7%), followed by aortic stenosis (3.1%), mitral regurgitation (2.0%), and aortic regurgitation (0.7%). Extrapolated to current US demographics, this implies at least 4.7 million people living with moderate VHD and 10.6 million with clinically significant VHD -- a substantially higher burden than prior clinic-based estimates, offering key epidemiological grounding for expanding screening programs.
