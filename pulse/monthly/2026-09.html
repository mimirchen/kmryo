Timing of PCI in Patients Undergoing Transcatheter Aortic-Valve Implantation: The TAVI PCI Trial
NEJM  ·  2026-09-03
The investigator-initiated TAVI PCI trial, presented as an ESC Congress 2026 Hot Line and published simultaneously in NEJM (Stähli et al.), randomized 986 patients with severe aortic stenosis and concomitant coronary artery disease across 48 centers in Austria, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, and Switzerland to a TAVI-first or PCI-first strategy, with the second procedure performed 1-45 days later. At 1 year, the composite primary endpoint (death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, ischemia-driven revascularization, valve/procedure/heart-failure-related rehospitalization, and severe bleeding) occurred in 22.2% of the TAVI-first group versus 24.2% of the PCI-first group (difference -2.0 percentage points, 95% CI -7.4 to 3.4; P<0.001 for noninferiority), with severe bleeding lower in the TAVI-first arm (6.6% vs 9.7%). The results support a TAVI-first sequencing strategy as a safe default when both procedures are needed.

TRICUSPID NEJM · 2026-09-03
Tricuspid-Valve Intervention in Heart Failure: The TRIC-I-HF Trial
The German multicenter TRIC-I-HF (TRIC-I-HF-DZHK24) trial, led by Joerg Hausleiter and colleagues across 29 high-volume heart-valve centers, randomized 360 patients (mean age 80 years, 56% women) with symptomatic severe tricuspid regurgitation and heart failure to transcatheter tricuspid valve repair plus medical therapy versus medical therapy alone. Presented at ESC Congress 2026 and published simultaneously in NEJM, the trial met its first hierarchical primary endpoint (win-ratio composite of death, heart-failure hospitalization, and quality-of-life improvement at 1 year), with a win ratio of 2.42 (95% CI 1.76-3.33; P<0.001) favoring transcatheter repair; the co-primary endpoint of freedom from death or heart-failure hospitalization at 3 years was 52.4% with repair versus 21.0% with medical therapy alone.
TRICUSPID JAMA · 2026-09-24
Real-World Outcomes of Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Analysis From the STS/ACC TVT Registry
Drawing on the US STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry, this study analyzed 1,034 consecutive patients (mean age 77, 69% female, 75% NYHA III/IV at baseline) who underwent transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR, EVOQUE system) at 82 US centers between February 2024 and March 2025. Tricuspid regurgitation was reduced to mild or less in 98.4% of patients post-procedure and 97.7% at 30 days; 30-day all-cause mortality was 3.1%, stroke 0.2%, major bleeding 7.9%, and heart failure hospitalization 3.1%, while 82.7% reached NYHA class I/II and mean KCCQ overall summary score improved by 22.4 points (all P<.001). The real-world registry results closely mirror the pivotal TRISCEND II trial, providing strong evidence supporting broader adoption of TTVR.
TAVI JAMA Cardiology · 2026-09-28
Seven-Year Valve Durability With Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement: An Ad Hoc Analysis of the PARTNER 3 Randomized Clinical Trial
In this ad hoc 7-year analysis of the PARTNER 3 randomized trial by Ternacle J, Hahn RT and the PARTNER 3 investigators, published in JAMA Cardiology, the authors followed 495 TAVR (SAPIEN 3) and 453 surgical bioprosthesis recipients among the original 1,000 low-surgical-risk patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis enrolled at 71 US/Canadian centers (March 2016-October 2017). At 7 years, rates of structural valve deterioration, all-cause bioprosthetic valve failure, and reintervention remained low and statistically similar between TAVR and surgery, providing the largest randomized dataset to date supporting comparable mid-to-long-term durability of the balloon-expandable valve versus surgical bioprostheses in low-risk, younger patients.
TAVI Circulation · 2026-09-09
Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement or Medical Treatment for Paradoxical Low-Flow, Low-Gradient Aortic Valve Stenosis: REBOOT-PARADOX
REBOOT-PARADOX is the first multicenter, open-label randomized trial dedicated to comparing TAVR plus optimal medical therapy (OMT) versus OMT alone in symptomatic paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient severe aortic stenosis (aortic valve area ≤ 1.0 cm², mean gradient < 40 mm Hg, stroke volume index < 35 mL/m², ejection fraction ≥ 50%), presented as an ESC Congress 2026 Hot Line and published in Circulation. Slow recruitment forced early termination, with only 120 of a planned 783 patients randomized (80 TAVR, 40 OMT; median age 82, 53.3% women), and 19 OMT patients crossing over to TAVR for symptom progression. All-cause death, the primary endpoint, occurred in 29.0% with TAVR versus 37.1% with OMT (HR 0.83; 95% CI, 0.41-1.66; P=0.60), and 5-year mortality was 44.9% versus 56.6% (HR 0.76; 95% CI, 0.43-1.35; P=0.35) — neither reaching statistical significance. The underpowered trial leaves TAVR's mortality benefit in this subgroup unresolved, supporting an individualized strategy of close surveillance with timely TAVR upon clinical deterioration, and underscores the need for an adequately powered trial.
TRICUSPID JACC: Advances · 2026-09-16
Prognostic Value of NYHA Functional Class in Tricuspid Valve Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair: Insights From EuroTR
The European EuroTR registry analyzed 3,467 patients undergoing tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER), with 3,424 having baseline NYHA functional class data. Two-year all-cause mortality was 28.4% (619/3,424; 95% CI 26.4%-30.4%), and each one-class increase in baseline NYHA class was associated with an 80% higher 2-year mortality risk (HR 1.8, 95% CI 1.5-2.1, p<0.001). Among 1,974 patients with paired follow-up data, the proportion in NYHA class I/II rose from 16.4% at baseline to 59.4% at follow-up, underscoring baseline symptom severity as a strong independent predictor of outcomes and supporting earlier referral for intervention.
TAVI JACC: Advances · 2026-09-20
Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement for Severe Bicuspid Aortic Valve Stenosis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
A systematic review and meta-analysis in JACC: Advances pooled 52 studies (~295,615 patients) comparing TAVR outcomes in bicuspid (BAV) versus tricuspid (TAV) aortic stenosis, and TAVR versus surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) in BAV patients. Versus TAV, TAVR in BAV carried higher risks of paravalvular leak (RR 1.44), aortic root injury (RR 1.83), 30-day stroke (RR 1.27) and 30-day mortality (RR 1.25), but lower 1-year mortality (RR 0.84). Versus SAVR in BAV patients, TAVR had significantly higher permanent pacemaker rates (OR 2.29), shorter hospital stays, and less bleeding (OR 0.31), with no significant difference in mortality, acute kidney injury, stroke, or vascular complications — supporting TAVR as feasible in carefully selected BAV anatomy at experienced centers.
TRICUSPID JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-09-21
Frequency and Outcomes of Acute Hemodynamic Instability Following Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Insights From the CESAR-TR-Registry
The international multicenter CESAR-TR registry retrospectively analyzed 200 high-surgical-risk patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation who underwent transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) across 5 centers (mean age 78±11 years, 64% women), examining the frequency, outcomes, and risk factors of acute hemodynamic instability (AHI) after the procedure. AHI occurred in 8.5% of patients, always within 24 hours post-procedure, and was associated with markedly higher in-hospital mortality (35.2% vs 0.1% without AHI; P<0.001); among AHI patients, 65% presented with SCAI stage C cardiogenic shock, 6% stage D, and 29% stage E. Elevated left-sided filling pressures, pulmonary hypertension, and impaired renal function independently predicted AHI risk, underscoring the importance of periprocedural hemodynamic monitoring and careful patient selection for TTVR.
TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-09-22
Surgical Bailout During TAVR: A Contemporary Analysis of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Database
Using the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) database, this contemporary US analysis examined 400,862 TAVR procedures performed between 2016 and 2024, of which 2,240 (0.56%) required emergent surgical bailout for procedural complications; the incidence fell significantly from 0.92% in 2016 to 0.40% in 2024. Despite this decline, outcomes after bailout remained poor: 30-day mortality was 26.3% versus 1.8% in patients who did not require surgery, with elevated mortality persisting at 1 year (35% vs 10%) and 5 years (48% vs 26%). The most common bailout interventions were mediastinal hematoma evacuation (38%), ventricular repair (20%), surgical aortic valve replacement (16%), and coronary artery bypass grafting (13%), underscoring the continued importance of heart-team readiness and on-site surgical backup even as TAVR technology matures.
TAVI JACC · 2026-09-30
10-Year Randomized Outcomes of Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement in Intermediate-Risk Aortic Stenosis
This 10-year re-analysis of the PARTNER 2A randomized trial, which assigned intermediate-risk aortic stenosis patients to TAVR with the second-generation balloon-expandable SAPIEN XT valve or surgery, found significantly higher all-cause mortality (86% vs 83%, HR 1.13, P=.02) and aortic valve reintervention (6% vs 2%, P<.001) with TAVR. Mortality was similar between transfemoral TAVR and surgery (84% vs 82%, P=.27), but significantly higher with transapical/transaortic TAVR versus surgery (93% vs 85%, P<.001; P for interaction=.03). A companion propensity-matched analysis using the newer third-generation SAPIEN 3 valve showed 10-year outcomes comparable to surgery, suggesting device and imaging-guided planning advances have narrowed the durability gap — but underscoring the long-term risks tied to non-transfemoral access with older-generation valves.
TAVI CMS · 2026-09-12
CMS Finalizes National Coverage Determination for TAVR, Expanding Medicare Coverage to Asymptomatic Severe Aortic Stenosis and Dropping the Two-Operator Mandate
On September 10, 2026, CMS finalized its updated National Coverage Determination (NCD) for TAVR after a nine-month review, removing Coverage with Evidence Development (CED) requirements for symptomatic severe aortic stenosis while extending Medicare coverage to asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis for the first time (still under CED registries), informed in part by EARLY TAVR trial data (15.2% vs 24.2% for the composite of death, stroke, or heart failure hospitalization). The policy also drops hospital procedural-volume thresholds and, for the first time, permits a single operator -- cardiologist or surgeon -- to perform TAVR without mandating both an interventional cardiologist and a cardiac surgeon, though hospitals must retain on-site cardiac surgery capability and the heart-team concept. The Society of Thoracic Surgeons voiced disappointment over the single-operator provision, while Edwards Lifesciences welcomed the update as expanding patient access, even as its shares dipped about 2.8% intraday on September 11 amid questions on adoption timelines. It is widely regarded as the most significant U.S. Medicare TAVR policy shift in over a decade.
TAVI JAMA · 2026-09-03
Anticoagulation Monotherapy vs Antiplatelet Monotherapy After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implant: The ACASA-TAVI Randomized Clinical Trial
The ACASA-TAVI randomized trial, presented at ESC Congress 2026 and published simultaneously in JAMA, enrolled 360 patients aged 65-80 undergoing TAVI for severe aortic stenosis with no other indication for anticoagulation, randomizing them to 12 months of direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) monotherapy versus aspirin 75 mg/day monotherapy. Hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT) on CT was less frequent with DOAC than with aspirin (16.2% vs 28.6%), while DOAC monotherapy was noninferior to aspirin for bleeding, thromboembolic events, and death, offering new randomized evidence on optimal antithrombotic strategy after TAVI.
TAVI JACC · 2026-09-02
Short-Term Anticoagulant Therapy and Subclinical Leaflet Thickening in Transcatheter Aortic Valves: The NOTION-4 Trial
The Danish NOTION-4 randomized trial, conducted at Rigshospitalet-Copenhagen University Hospital and Aarhus University Hospital and led by Troels Højsgaard Jørgensen and Ole De Backer, enrolled 324 TAVI patients with no standing indication for oral anticoagulation, randomizing them to a 3-month direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) course followed by lifelong single antiplatelet therapy versus lifelong single antiplatelet therapy alone. The primary endpoint was 1-year cardiac 4DCT-assessed hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT). DOAC therapy significantly reduced HALT incidence compared with antiplatelet therapy alone (RR 0.50, 95% CI 0.36-0.70, P=0.0001). Presented in an ESC Congress 2026 Hot Line session and published simultaneously in JACC, the trial adds new randomized evidence on periprocedural antithrombotic strategy for TAVI patients without a standing anticoagulation indication, complementing the concurrently reported ACASA-TAVI trial in patients already needing anticoagulation.
TAVI JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-09-04
Redo-Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement With Self-Expanding Valves: A U.S. Registry Analysis
Using the STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy (TVT) Registry, Rogers, Bapat and colleagues analyzed 439 patients undergoing redo-TAVR for a failed TAVR valve, all treated with supra-annular self-expanding Evolut R/PRO/PRO+ valves (mean age 78.9±9.8 years, 63.1% women), compared with a contemporary native-TAVR cohort on the same valve platform. After adjustment, redo-TAVR showed mortality and stroke rates comparable to native TAVR at both 30 days (mortality HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.39-1.13; stroke HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.54-1.68) and 1 year (mortality HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.72-1.15; stroke HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.63-1.34). Published August 24, 2026, in JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, it is the largest real-world dataset to date on rescue strategies for TAVR valve failure.
TAVI JACC: Asia · 2026-09-18
Ten-Year Clinical Outcomes and Valve Durability After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation With Balloon-Expandable Valves
A Japanese multicenter registry analysis followed 297 patients who received early-generation SAPIEN XT balloon-expandable valves between 2010 and 2014 (mean age 83.8±6.0 years, STS score 7.5±4.0) for up to 10 years to assess long-term outcomes and valve durability. Freedom from all-cause mortality at 10 years was 13.2%, while cumulative bioprosthetic valve failure per VARC-3 criteria rose from 0.8% at 1 year to 3.6% at 5 years and 10.8% at 10 years, with severe structural valve deterioration in 3.0% and reintervention required in 4.5%. The findings suggest reassuring long-term durability for early-generation balloon-expandable valves, with 10-year mortality driven mainly by comorbidities rather than valve dysfunction.
