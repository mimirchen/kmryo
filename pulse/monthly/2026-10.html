Real-World Outcomes of Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement: Analysis From the STS/ACC TVT Registry
JAMA  ·  2026-10-16
A retrospective STS/ACC TVT Registry analysis led by Makkar and colleagues, published in JAMA, examined 1,034 patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR) treated with the EVOQUE transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) system at 82 US centers between February 2024 and March 2025 (mean age 77.1 years, 69.1% women). Procedural success exceeded 98%, and more than 97% of patients had mild or lesser TR at 30 days. Thirty-day rates were 3.1% for all-cause mortality, 0.2% for stroke, and 7.9% for severe bleeding -- bleeding and new permanent pacemaker rates both came in lower than in the pivotal TRISCEND II randomized trial (10.4% severe bleeding). The authors cautioned that the registry lacked a concurrent comparator arm and core-laboratory echocardiographic adjudication, so TTVR's long-term durability and real-world benefit still need longer follow-up.

IMAGING JAMA Cardiology · 2026-10-17
Artificial Intelligence–Enabled Acquisition and Interpretation for Screening Aortic Stenosis
A Mayo Clinic team (Lee, Naser, Kane, and colleagues) reported in JAMA Cardiology the development and validation of an AI system that both guides probe acquisition and automatically interprets focused cardiac ultrasound (FoCUS) images to screen for moderate-or-greater aortic stenosis. The algorithm was first validated on 602 exams acquired by experienced sonographers, then tested prospectively on 1,302 exams acquired by nine research staff with no prior clinical or ultrasound experience who received only four hours of training. For detecting moderate-or-greater aortic stenosis, sensitivity was 85.4%, specificity 99.7%, positive predictive value 91.1%, and negative predictive value 99.4%. A two-step workflow routing AI-positive or technically inadequate studies to expert review further improved positive predictive value while requiring expert review of only about 10% of exams. The authors frame the tool as a community screening aid rather than a diagnostic replacement, aimed at widening early detection of aortic stenosis.
TAVI JAMA Cardiology · 2026-10-18
Seven-Year Valve Durability With Transcatheter or Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement: An Ad Hoc Analysis of the PARTNER 3 Randomized Clinical Trial
An ad hoc analysis of the PARTNER 3 randomized trial, led by Julien Ternacle and colleagues and published in JAMA Cardiology, compared 7-year valve durability between 495 low-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis who received balloon-expandable SAPIEN 3 TAVR and 453 who underwent surgical aortic valve replacement. Structural valve deterioration, endocarditis-related valve dysfunction (0.4% with TAVR vs 0.5% with surgery), and all-cause bioprosthetic valve failure requiring reintervention were low and similar between groups; freedom from all-cause valve failure among survivors at 7 years was 73% with TAVR versus 75% with surgery. Thrombosis-related valve dysfunction was more common early after TAVR but was mostly reversible and rarely progressed to failure or reintervention. An accompanying editorial called the findings reassuring evidence of durable long-term TAVR performance in low-risk patients.
MITRAL JACC: Advances · 2026-10-02
Mitral Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair in Ventricular Functional Mitral Regurgitation and Very Severe Left Ventricular Dysfunction
The Japanese OCEAN-Mitral registry enrolled 1,538 patients with ventricular functional mitral regurgitation and LVEF ≤40% undergoing M-TEER, comparing those with LVEF <20% (n=103) against LVEF 20%-40% (n=1,435); acute procedural success was similarly high (100% vs 97%) and in-hospital mortality did not differ (5% vs 4%), but LVEF <20% independently predicted higher 1-year cardiovascular mortality (HR 2.00, 95% CI 1.08-3.68). Although such severely impaired patients have largely been excluded from randomized trials, this largest-to-date registry shows M-TEER remains feasible and brings meaningful symptomatic benefit without excess death or heart-failure hospitalization.
TAVI JACC: Advances · 2026-10-04
Long-Term Outcomes After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement in Nonagenarians: Impact of Frailty and Malnutrition
A Japanese multicenter registry study published in JACC: Advances (March 2026) followed 4,623 transfemoral TAVR patients, including 700 aged ≥90 years, for 5 years. All-cause mortality was significantly higher in nonagenarians (53.2% vs 37.0%, P<0.001), driven mainly by noncardiovascular deaths (32.5% vs 19.9%, P<0.001), while cardiovascular mortality was similar between groups (20.3% vs 17.0%, P=0.198). On multivariable analysis, age ≥90 itself was not an independent mortality predictor -- frailty and malnutrition were the strongest determinants of long-term outcomes, suggesting TAVR candidacy should be guided by geriatric assessment rather than chronological age alone.
LAA JACC: Clinical Electrophysiology · 2026-10-07
Outcomes of Patients With Reduced Left Ventricular Function Undergoing Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion
A large NCDR LAAO Registry analysis identified 186,085 patients undergoing left atrial appendage occlusion with documented LVEF between December 2015 and December 2023, including 5,192 (2.8%) with LVEF <30%. Procedural success (98.0% vs 98.1%, P=0.12) and in-hospital mortality (adjusted OR 1.39, 95% CI 0.76-2.55) were similar between patients with LVEF <30% and LVEF ≥50%, and 1-year stroke rates did not differ significantly. However, all-cause mortality at 1 year rose significantly with every 10% drop in LVEF, with the highest risk in the LVEF <30% group (adjusted HR 1.93, 95% CI 1.75-2.12), suggesting severely reduced LV function should not by itself preclude LAAO despite its link to higher longer-term mortality.
TAVI JACC: Advances · 2026-10-08
Antiplatelet Therapy and All-Cause Mortality After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement: The TRITAVI Registry
The multicenter TRITAVI registry analyzed 10,071 patients undergoing TAVR, with a focused comparison of single (SAPT) versus dual (DAPT) antiplatelet therapy in a femoral-access subgroup (a SCAI-presented subset included 5,514 patients: 3,197 on SAPT, 2,317 on DAPT). SAPT was associated with lower 6-month all-cause mortality (2.4% vs 5.4% with DAPT) and less major bleeding (0.5% vs 1.3%); after adjustment, DAPT carried a significantly higher 6-month mortality risk (HR 1.69, 95% CI 1.20-2.37) that persisted at 24 months (HR 1.21, 95% CI 1.01-1.46). The findings suggest single antiplatelet therapy may lower both death and bleeding risk compared with dual therapy in TAVR patients without an indication for long-term anticoagulation, though the authors caution this is observational registry evidence requiring prospective randomized confirmation.
TRICUSPID JACC: Advances · 2026-10-08
Temporal Trends in Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Interventions After FDA Approval in the United States
A U.S. national cohort analysis tracked adoption trends in transcatheter tricuspid edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) and transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) following FDA approval of tricuspid devices. Monthly T-TEER volumes rose from 128 cases in October 2024 to 156 in December 2025, while TTVR volumes grew from 60 to 111 over the same period. In-hospital mortality was lower with T-TEER (0.5%) than TTVR (2.0%), and in-hospital major bleeding was likewise lower (6.0% vs 12.4%), offering real-world evidence to inform procedure selection as tricuspid device use expands.
TAVI JACC · 2026-10-16
10-Year Outcomes of SAPIEN 3 Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement or Surgery in Intermediate-Risk Patients
A propensity-matched analysis published in the June 16, 2026 JACC focus issue compared TAVR patients from the PARTNER 2 SAPIEN 3 Intermediate-Risk registry (P2S3i) with surgical patients from the PARTNER 2A trial: 2,005 patients (1,069 TAVR, 936 surgery) were analyzed, yielding 783 matched pairs. At 10 years, all-cause mortality was similar (83.4% TAVR vs. 82.3% surgery; HR 1.01, 95% CI 0.91-1.13, P=0.82), as was aortic valve reintervention (2.0% vs. 1.9%, P=0.47). That contrasts with a companion PARTNER 2A analysis of the earlier second-generation SAPIEN XT valve published in the same issue, where 10-year all-cause mortality was higher with TAVR (86% vs. 83% surgery; HR 1.13, P=0.02) and reintervention was also higher (6% vs. 2%, P<0.001). Together the two papers suggest that newer balloon-expandable valve design may have closed the late-term gap seen with earlier-generation transcatheter valves versus surgery, though experts caution that high background mortality among elderly patients at 10 years complicates the comparison, and durability conclusions still hinge on longer follow-up of newer valve platforms.
TAVI ACC · 2026-10-14
CMS Finalizes Updated TAVR National Coverage Determination
In September 2026, CMS finalized an updated national coverage determination (NCD) for TAVR: symptomatic severe aortic stenosis patients are now covered without the prior coverage-with-evidence-development (CED) requirement, while asymptomatic severe AS remains covered only under CMS-approved CED studies -- none of which currently exist, so that population is not yet actually covered. The policy also drops the long-standing dual-operator mandate (letting the heart team decide whether an interventional cardiologist and a cardiac surgeon must jointly perform each case), eliminates hospital TAVR volume thresholds, and removes mandatory registry participation as a coverage condition (hospitals must still run a continuous quality-improvement process). ACC, STS, and SCAI issued a joint statement welcoming the update as better matching TAVR's current role as a cornerstone therapy for severe aortic stenosis.
TAVI EuroIntervention · 2026-10-09
Long-Term Structural Valve Deterioration After TAVI: Insights From the EORP-ESC Valve Durability TAVI Registry
The EORP-ESC Valve Durability TAVI Registry, coordinated across 22 European centres under the ESC's EurObservational Research Programme, followed 597 surviving TAVI patients (mean age 79.6±7.1 years at implantation, 47.2% male) with echocardiography within 6 months of enrollment and again at 5+ years, grading structural valve deterioration (SVD) by VARC-3 criteria. At a median follow-up of 6.1 years (IQR 5.2-7.3), the crude incidence of moderate/severe SVD was 9.5% (n=57; moderate 6.2%, severe 3.4%). Multivariable analysis identified three independent predictors: intra-annular valve design (HR 38.44), small valve size (HR 4.82), and moderate-to-severe paravalvular leak (HR 3.64). The authors say the findings argue for caution when choosing older intra-annular designs in younger TAVI candidates, while cautioning that this is observational registry data with only 61% of echocardiograms read by a core laboratory.
MITRAL Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions · 2026-10-08
Single TriClip Steerable Guide for Combined MitraClip and TriClip Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair (STriC-TEER): A Multicenter Experience
A multicenter retrospective study from Hong Kong, Taiwan, Thailand, and Australia reports STriC-TEER, a technique using a single TriClip steerable guide catheter (SGC) to deliver both the MitraClip system for mitral edge-to-edge repair and, after withdrawing the same guide into the right atrium, the TriClip system for tricuspid repair -- avoiding the catheter exchange required in conventional combined procedures. The series included 40 patients treated between 2023 and 2025 (mean age 76.8±9.1 years, 70% male, 97.1% with atrial fibrillation), four of whom also underwent concurrent left atrial appendage occlusion. The authors call the single-guide strategy safe and feasible, while noting that prospective randomized trials are needed to compare it with the conventional catheter-exchange approach for efficiency, outcomes, and cost-effectiveness.
TAVI EHJ - Valvular and Structural Heart Disease · 2026-10-12
Challenges in the Timely Management of Severe Aortic Stenosis: A Multicentre Analysis of Delayed Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation Referrals and Gender Disparities
A multicentre observational study in the European Heart Journal - Valvular and Structural Heart Disease analyzed 519 patients (46.1% women) referred for TAVI with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis across three European centres in 2023. Women experienced substantially longer diagnostic delays from symptom onset to diagnosis than men (roughly 3 months vs 1 month), with marked variation between centres, yet once referred, time from diagnosis to TAVI and post-procedural outcomes were similar between sexes -- suggesting the sex gap in aortic stenosis care lies mainly in disease recognition and referral rather than in the TAVI pathway itself.
TRICUSPID JACC: Asia · 2026-10-01
Asian Pacific Society of Cardiology Consensus Statements on the Use of Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair in the Treatment of Tricuspid and Mitral Regurgitation
The Asian Pacific Society of Cardiology (APSC) has issued its first regional consensus statement on transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) for tricuspid (TR) and mitral regurgitation (MR), authored by an international panel including Yeo KK, Muller DWM and Wang JA, comprising 16 recommendations. The statement calls for multidisciplinary Heart Team evaluation and rigorous imaging-based anatomic screening (leaflet mobility, annular size, coaptation) before TEER, noting that current evidence shows TEER reduces symptoms, improves quality of life and lowers heart-failure hospitalizations, offering a safe option for surgery-ineligible patients with severe TR or MR. The document addresses a gap for the Asia-Pacific region, where patient anatomy and device access can differ from Western cohorts.
MITRAL JACC: Advances · 2026-10-04
Sex-Specific Outcomes in Patients Undergoing Mitral Valve Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair: The REPAIR Study
The REPAIR study, published in JACC: Advances on May 13, 2026, analyzed 2,601 patients undergoing PASCAL-system transcatheter edge-to-edge mitral repair (M-TEER), including 1,150 women (44.2%) and 1,451 men (55.8%). Women achieved an "optimal result" less often than men (55.7% vs 65.9%, P<0.001), driven by a higher rate of elevated postprocedural transmitral gradients (≥5 mmHg: 24.0% vs 14.0%, P<0.001), while residual mild-or-less MR rates and technical success (97.0% vs 97.7%) were similar. One-year all-cause mortality or heart failure hospitalization did not differ significantly by sex (20.1% vs 20.8%), suggesting comparable overall safety and benefit despite smaller annular anatomy predisposing women to higher residual gradients.
